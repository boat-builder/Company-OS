## Section 2 — The services-for-equity deal at the $5M angel cap: structure and math

### The right structure (Vehicle #1 of 3)

**Invoice-then-convert SAFE.** Agency invoices Berlin monthly at agreed notional rate. Berlin doesn't pay cash; unpaid balance accrues; on quarterly cadence (or at next priced round), accumulated balance converts into a post-money SAFE at the **same $5M cap as the angel round** (NOT the $10M seed cap). Survives VC diligence, lets you throttle exposure, only the accrued portion converts if engagement ends early. (Cooley GO and Wilson Sonsini both have SAFE templates that take a "services consideration" rider.)

### Anchor: $5/hr internal rate, 0.2% for 2,000 hours

At the angel round terms ($50K = 1%, effective $5M cap), every percentage point = $50K of value:

| Equity | $ value | Hours @ $5/hr (anchor) | Hours @ $7/hr (cost-plus) | Hours @ $10/hr (his probable ask) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10% | $5,000 | 1,000 | 714 | 500 |
| **0.20% (anchor)** | **$10,000** | **2,000** | 1,429 | 1,000 |
| 0.30% | $15,000 | 3,000 | 2,143 | 1,500 |
| 0.40% | $20,000 | 4,000 | 2,857 | 2,000 |
| **0.50% (walk-away wall)** | **$25,000** | **5,000** | 3,571 | 2,500 |
| 0.75% | $37,500 | 7,500 | 5,357 | 3,750 |
| 1.00% | $50,000 | 10,000 | 7,143 | 5,000 |

**What 2,000 hours actually buys:** ~1 PT FDM (40hrs/wk × 50 weeks) for 12 months, OR ~1 FT FDM for 6 months, OR a split bundle (1 PT FDM + 1 PT writer + occasional dev help over 12 months). Real, useful capacity — not token.

### Why $5/hr is defensible (the framing that wins)

He pays the FDM ~$2–3/hr, has another ~$1–2/hr in admin/office overhead, so $5/hr gives him meaningful margin (~50–100% over fully-loaded cost). That's the standard "cost-plus" range for agency portfolio-resourcing arrangements (Betaworks, Expa precedents).

**The rhetorical move when he tries to inflate the rate:** *"This is an investment, not a service purchase. Cash investors don't get paid market interest on their capital — they take equity in exchange for foregone returns. Your foregone return is the hours you could have billed clients at; we compensate that with equity upside, not by also paying market rate. If you want billable rate, that's a vendor contract, not an investment — and there's no equity. We have to pick one."*

### Walk-away wall: 0.5% for the services component

Hard cap. Reasoning that's also clean to say out loud: *"The most this round gives anyone is 0.5% — that's a $25K check at $50K-for-1% terms. I can't price your services higher than I'd price someone else's cash."*

**Walk-away triggers (any one = no deal):**

1. He demands more than 0.5% for services worth ≤$25K notional.
2. He insists on billable-rate framing ($15–25/hr) AND equity. (Pick one.)
3. He pushes for a cap BELOW $5M (e.g., "give me a $3M cap on my services tranche because services are riskier than cash"), OR keeps the $5M cap but inflates rate/hours to extract effective sub-$5M pricing. The cash angels in this round are at $5M; pricing his services more generously than that breaks fairness with them and creates a cap-table problem that surfaces in the seed.
4. He attaches commercial restrictions (exclusivity, customer non-solicit on Berlin, forced Berlin-for-Agencies discount).
5. He asks for governance rights (board observer, info rights, ROFR). A $10–25K-equivalent angel doesn't get those at any size of angel round.

### Defending against the "doesn't know the market" inflation pressure

If he pushes for higher % because he's anchoring on agency-billing instincts (not investor instincts), three moves in increasing firmness:

1. **Educate gently.** "The going rate for an angel round at this stage in AI-native SaaS is $5M–$10M post. We're at $5M effectively, which is already half the seed price. Going lower than that is below market by a lot."
2. **Reframe to your opportunity cost.** "I'm giving up 1% of the company for $50K total in this round. That's the most expensive money I'll ever take. If I price the round differently for you because services feel different, I have to re-explain it to every other angel — and that conversation doesn't end well."
3. **Anchor to fairness with cash investors.** "If [other angel name] sees you got 1% for $25K-equivalent of services, they'll renegotiate or pull. The clean rule is: services and cash get priced the same way. That's the only structure that survives."

### Plan B — what to do if he won't come close to the anchor

In rough order of preference:

**1. Decline politely.** Best option if the gap is wide. You have $25K committed of $50K already. $25K is not load-bearing — finding another angel for $25K cash is faster and cleaner than burning weeks on a deal that distorts the cap table. Walk-away language: *"This round is small and I'm trying to keep the cap table simple — let's revisit at the seed round when there's more room and we can structure resources separately."*

**2. Reframe as commercial-only — no equity.** Berlin pays the agency contractor rates ($7–10/hr cost-plus) for resources, AND offers the Berlin-for-Agencies discount (25–35% band) as the relationship sweetener. Real economic value to him (margin on the work + discounted access for his own clients) without a competitor on the cap table at the wrong price. **Best fallback if he genuinely wants resources to flow but the equity math doesn't pencil.**

**3. Tiny pilot, no equity, no commitment.** ~100–300 hours of one resource (a writer, not an FDM — easier to evaluate, lower training cost) at internal cost. No equity, 60-day window. Tests the agency's actual quality, his behavior as a partner, keeps the relationship warm. Re-open the equity conversation only if the pilot is clearly working.

**4. Defer to the seed round at $10M cap.** "Let's not stretch the angel round to fit. Come into the seed at $10M cap if you're still keen — your $25K services would be 0.25%, smaller bite for both of us, and we can structure the services arrangement properly with a vesting rider on the SAFE."

**5. Partial check + smaller services.** If he REALLY wants in but his services-only framing is too rich: ask for a small cash component ($5–10K) plus a smaller services component (~$5K notional). Total stays at ≤0.3%. Splits the risk and signals real commitment.

### What NOT to do

- Don't take $25K services for 0.5% if it requires accepting his rack-rate framing — establishes a precedent that infects every future angel conversation.
- Don't price the resource SAFE at the $10M seed cap to "make it work" — breaks the angel-round discount logic and creates an explainability problem with the cash angels who paid $5M-cap pricing.
- Don't blend the equity discussion with the Berlin-for-Agencies discount in the same negotiation pass. If equity gets contentious, *that's* when you pull the discount out as a separate consolation prize.

### Opening offer (verbatim)

> "Here's how I'd want to structure this so it's clean for both of us. The angel round is sized at $50K total for 1% — I priced it at half the seed cap as a discount for trusting us early. So the simplest way to do this: your agency invoices us monthly at $5/hour, which is a healthy markup over your fully-loaded cost on these people. The unpaid balance accrues on a services rider to a SAFE at the same $5M cap as the cash angels in this round, converting quarterly as your team actually delivers. For 2,000 hours of services — roughly one part-time FDM for a year, or a mixed bundle of a part-time FDM, a writer, and some dev time — that's $10K of services and 0.2% of the company. The most we'd ever go on this round, even at the higher end, is 0.5% — same as a $25K cash check at this round's terms. We name the specific people, your team signs IP assignment to Berlin per individual, and if you reassign anyone we have 14 days to approve the replacement or that month doesn't convert. Does that shape work for you?"

### Failure modes to defend against contractually

- **Resource swap-out** (A-team to win, juniors after) → name the FDMs in the contract; Berlin sign-off on replacements.
- **Time-share dilution** ("dedicated" FDMs split across the agency's clients) → monthly timesheet attestation; kill-switch on conversion if utilization < 80%.
- **Customer poaching** → 24-month customer non-solicit on the agency entity.
- **Competing IP capture** → per-individual NDA + IP assignment; "Berlin methodology" definition clause.
- **Cap-table overhang** → hard cap deal size at ~1.5% additional dilution.
- **Misaligned exit incentives** → standard YC post-money SAFE; no special preference, no special transfer rights.

### Critical IP language

Default agency contracts say agency retains IP until paid. For services-for-equity that's a disaster (FDMs touch customer data, build playbooks on Berlin's substrate). Required: **work-for-hire with full IP assignment to Berlin on creation, not on payment**, plus PIIA-equivalent clause each individual FDM signs (not just the agency entity).

### Future-round perception

Services-for-equity *from a competitor agency* is a yellow flag for Series A VCs. Manageable if: vanilla post-money SAFE, no commercial attachments (exclusive supply, channel rights, customer non-solicit), no governance rights. If the agency tries to attach commercial terms, kill the deal.

## Section 3 — The Berlin-for-Agencies discount + side leverage

### Discount band (your opening anchor for letting his agency use Berlin's tech for their own clients)

- **Math floor:** ~50% off list. Below that, you're underwriting his customer acquisition with your equity.
- **Market band reference:** standard reseller 15–25%; premier/volume 25–35%; white-label 35–50%.
- **Strategic premium owed here:** he's risking opportunity cost on billable hours; multi-client workload generates 5–10× case-study velocity; agency-channel motion already has precedent (the 2.5M-page flagship).

**Recommendation:**
- **Open at 25% off list** ("standard channel partner pricing — same as we'd offer any agency walking in cold").
- **Target: 35–40% off list,** locked 18 months, tied to volume commitment (5 paid client seats by M6, 10 by M12). Below volume floor, discount steps back to 25%.
- **Floor: 50% off list,** only with bundled commitments (geographic exclusivity 12 months, or 12-month case study + co-marketing commitment).
- **Walk below 50%.**

**Structural notes:**
- Discount applies to **platform list price only**, not LLM pass-through (those stay at cost — non-negotiable).
- **MFN-back clause:** if Berlin offers any other agency a deeper discount on equivalent volume, he gets matched. (Comfort without giving away the future.)
- **No MFN-forward** — he doesn't get future product additions at this rate.
- Discount renews annually subject to volume; no perpetual lock.
- Carve out enterprise-tier seats (Heavy/Enterprise customers) to a smaller discount (15–20%).

### Training transfer (leverage you OFFER)

His people come back upskilled on AI-native SEO/AEO. Real value: $15K–$25K/year/head retained delivery capacity. To Berlin, mostly absorbed cost (training FDMs anyway). **Real risk: IP leakage** (playbooks, prompts, FDM workflows, Snake.blue patterns).

Protect with layered contract:
1. **Tiered IP separation** — operators see *outputs and outcomes*, not playbook files, prompt libraries, internal docs.
2. **12-month non-replication clause** — for 12 months post-engagement, the agency cannot productize an "AI-native SEO" offering that mirrors Berlin's substrate-anchored playbook (define narrowly so it's enforceable).
3. **Tooling stays in Berlin's environment** — no exports of internal scripts or training corpus.
4. **Don't price the training separately.** Position as benefit included in equity terms — preserves your right to withhold deeper IP from future agency deals.

### Post-deal continuity option (leverage you OFFER, but he wants it)

Turns his resource investment into ongoing revenue post-engagement. Structure as **right of first refusal**, not obligation: for 12 months after equity engagement ends, if Berlin needs incremental FDM capacity, his agency gets first look at agreed rates.

- **Rate guidance:** market contractor rates for senior SEO operators (NOT Indian internal cost basis — different geography), with 10–15% loyalty discount in exchange for priority access.
- **Termination triggers:** quality SLA misses, pricing escalation above agreed band, agency launches directly competing AI-native SEO offering.
- **Option period:** 12 months, renewable by mutual agreement. Don't lock in perpetuity.
- **Volume cap** (e.g., "up to N FDM-equivalent hours/month") so you don't get stuck if their rates drift uncompetitive.

### Negotiation choreography for the meeting

1. **Lead with equity terms and resource-investment structure.** Settle the principal terms first. Don't muddy with side leverage until the core feels close.
2. **Introduce post-deal continuity SECOND, as mutual benefit.** "Beyond this engagement, if it's going well, we'd want first dibs on continuing with your operators — paid at your normal rates." Generous-sounding hedge.
3. **Introduce training transfer THIRD, as your concession.** "Your people will come out trained on the most current AI-native SEO playbook in market." Frame as goodwill, don't price.
4. **Introduce Berlin-for-Agencies discount LAST. Let HIM ask for it if you can.** If he asks: anchor 25% with "standard channel partner pricing" framing. Walk down through the band — 30%, 35% — extracting volume commitment and case-study rights at each step.
5. **If he opens with the discount ask first** (likely if he's done his homework): anchor at 25%, then bring training and continuity in as *additions* on top — making the package feel rich without moving on the discount itself.
6. **Hold the LLM-pass-through line firmly.** Use it as the rhetorical "thing I cannot move on" so other concessions feel earned.

## Section 4 — Conversation tree: directions this can go

### a) Standard friendly investor
Signal: peer-to-peer curiosity, asks about FDM bottleneck, asks "what unlocks the most for you in 90 days?"
Move: "FDM capacity at M6–M9. Let me walk you through what 'one FDM' actually costs me to bring online — about 4 weeks of my own time end-to-end — and then you tell me which of your people you'd lend, for how long, and what you want in return. Co-design the structure rather than me pitch a number."

### b) The reseller play (most likely)
Signal: keeps steering to Berlin-for-Agencies, asks about white-label, multi-client governance, pricing for his book. Equity conversation short, access conversation long.
Move: Treat as commercial deal in equity clothing. "It sounds like the immediate value is running Berlin underneath your delivery. Let's separate the two — what would a normal Berlin-for-Agencies engagement look like at standard pricing, and then we can talk about whether the resource swap makes sense as a separate equity conversation. I don't want you paying equity prices for what should be a contract."

### c) The skill-transfer play (most likely)
Signal: emphasizes "my people will learn so much," wants visibility into playbooks, pushes for senior people to shadow you. Vague on which people, specific on what they'd be exposed to.
Move: Name it openly. "A meaningful chunk of what you'd get is your team learning the AI-native delivery model from inside. That has real value to your agency independent of Berlin's outcome. I want the equity to match resource hours actually delivered to Berlin, not the training value you take home. Want to walk through how we'd separate those two?"

### d) Acqui-hire scout
Signal: 3-year picture questions, exit timing, who else is on cap table. Less interest in operational mechanics.
Move: "Right now I'm building toward $1.1M ARR M12, CFP M8 — not optimizing for an acquisition window. If down the line that conversation makes sense, the cleanest way to be in it is as a cash investor with normal pro-rata, not a resource investor with extra rights."

### e) Information extraction
Signal: detailed questions about data routing, Snake.blue, FDM training playbook, prompts, vendor mix. Investment terms keep getting deferred.
Move: "I'm going to stay at architecture level rather than implementation level for now — substrate is the moat, I treat it that way with everyone pre-term-sheet. Once we have a draft structure on paper I'll happily go deeper. What would it take to get to a one-page term outline this week?"

### f) The lowball
Signal: 5–10% for "a couple of writers part-time," anchors resource value at billable rate, dismisses the $10M cap.
Move: Don't get defensive about the cap. Make him do the math. "A trained FDM costs me about [X weeks of my time + $Y]. A content writer at the level we need is roughly $Z/mo fully loaded. So your resources map to about $N total annual value. At a $10M cap that's Y%. If your math comes out differently, walk me through how you're valuing the resources."

### g) The overcommit
Signal: 2 FDMs, 4 writers, a developer, all next month. Sounds generous; lands as capacity poison pill.
Move: Flatter, then scope to your ramp curve. "More generous than I can responsibly absorb in the first six months — I personally train every resource and I'd rather train two well than six badly. Let's structure in tranches: tranche 1 is one FDM and one writer through M6, tranche 2 unlocks against [milestone] and your team being available. Equity vests against tranches actually delivered."

### h) The control grab
Signal: board observer, monthly KPI dump, ROFR, anti-dilution, vendor exclusivity.
Move: "For a SAFE at this stage and check size, I'm holding to standard YC SAFE terms: no board, no info rights beyond annual update, no ROFR, no MFN beyond standard. Resource component is a separate vesting schedule. If structure needs more rights to work for you, we should reshape this as something else entirely."

### i) Conditional on cash co-invest (chicken-and-egg)
Signal: "I'll commit my resources once you close cash" — or inverse.
Move: "If you're a yes once cash closes, write me a soft-circle on the same terms now — that helps me run the cash conversations faster, which helps your timing too. If you'd actually be a faster yes if I had cash signal, I can share where the angel round stands [$25K committed of $50K]. Which way is more honest about what you need to see?"

### j) He pivots and offers cash
Signal: late: "Actually I could write a check too — would that be useful?"
Move: Yes — and reshape so cash is primary, resources are a separate friends-and-family services arrangement. "I'd actually rather take it that way. Cash on the SAFE at the $10M cap, then we treat any resource help as a normal services arrangement at a friends-and-family rate — cleaner for both of us, keeps your cap table position simple, avoids the conflict-of-interest mess of a competitor on the cap table doing delivery work. Want me to redraft that way?"

### k) Wildcards
- *White-label Berlin under his brand* → hard no on white-label, soft yes on Berlin-for-Agencies as co-branded delivery layer.
- *JV in his geography* → tempting but locks Berlin out of direct motion later. Counter with a referral arrangement, not a JV.
- *Migrate his agency's clients onto Berlin as part of deal* → separate commercial track, requires customer consent. Decouple.
