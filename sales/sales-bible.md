## What Berlin Is (Positioning)

Berlin is an **end-to-end AI SEO/AEO platform**. The user talks to a dashboard; Berlin strategizes, sets up execution, and executes. The mental model is three pillars — **Strategy / Execution / Tools** — and Berlin owns all three.

- The user can optionally bring their own strategy and let Berlin execute.
- USOL (the underlying orchestration/inference stack) is internal infrastructure. It is **not** a standalone product and should not be pitched as one.
- The alternative Berlin replaces is **hiring an SEO agency or building an in-house SEO team** — not a workflow tool, not a point solution. When prospects compare us to Ahrefs, SEMrush, Clearscope, etc., gently reframe: those are tools in the stack Berlin already operates; the real decision is "who does our SEO."

### One-line pitch
"Berlin is an end-to-end AI SEO/AEO platform. Instead of hiring an agency or building an in-house team, you talk to Berlin's dashboard — it handles strategy, execution, and the tools underneath."

## Qualifying Questions — Placing the Prospect in the Grid

A good discovery call does three things: identifies the **archetype**, identifies the **buyer persona**, and maps the **current state** (DIY / agency / in-house / nothing).

### 1. Archetype — what kind of business?
**Ask:** "Tell me about the business — what do you sell, and how do you make money?"

Listen for:
- **SaaS** — recurring software revenue, product-led or sales-led.
- **eCommerce** — physical or digital goods sold direct (Shopify, DTC, marketplaces-adjacent).
- **Lead Gen / Services** — B2B services, local services, consultancies, law/finance/health. SEO drives pipeline.

If they're none of the above (marketplace, publisher, aggregator, pure agency), note it — likely out of scope.

### 2. Buyer persona — Founder or Marketing Leader?
**Ask:** "What's your role in the business, and who owns SEO today?"

| Founder tier ($499–$999) | Marketing Leader tier ($2.5k–$5k) |
|---|---|
| "I'm the founder, I do it myself" | "I'm the Head of Marketing / CMO / Demand Gen Lead" |
| "We're 2–15 people, no dedicated marketer" | "We're 20–100+, I have a team / agency already" |
| "I sign the checks personally" | "I have a budget line for SEO" |
| Willing to sit in the product | Wants it done, wants reports, wants accountability |

If ambiguous, ask directly: "Do you want to run SEO yourself with Berlin doing the heavy lifting, or do you want Berlin to run it and just show you results?"

### 3. Current state — what's in place today?
**Ask:** "What's happening with SEO right now — who does it and how?"

| State | Signal | Positioning angle |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| **Nothing / DIY** | Founder doing it at night, blog posts occasionally | Berlin replaces the need to hire; you get strategy + execution from day one |
| **Freelancer(s)** | "Hired someone on Upwork," inconsistent results | Berlin is more reliable than a freelancer and includes strategy |
| **Agency** | $3k–$10k/mo retainer, unhappy, opaque reporting | **The core wedge.** Berlin replaces the agency at comparable or lower cost, with more transparency and faster iteration |
| **In-house SEO person/team** | Dedicated hire(s) | Berlin augments them; or, at earlier stage, replaces the need for the next hire |
| **Tools only** | Ahrefs/SEMrush subscriptions but no execution | Those are tools *inside* Berlin's stack — we go further, all the way to execution |

### 4. Trigger — why now?
**Ask:** "What's making you look at this now rather than six months ago or six months from now?"

Good triggers:
- Agency contract renewal coming up
- Traffic/ranking drop (often AI-overview related in 2026)
- AI search anxiety (ChatGPT / Perplexity / Google AI Overviews)
- Funding round → growth targets
- Competitor outranking them
- Freelancer/agency burn, no trust left

Bad triggers: "Just exploring," "Someone mentioned you," "Curious about AI."

### 5. Budget sanity
**Ask:** "What are you spending on SEO today, across tools, freelancers, and agency or in-house?"

Map their answer to the tier their persona implies:
- Founder tier: they should be spending $200–$2,000/mo today (tools + occasional freelancer). If $0, low likelihood of closing at $499–$999.
- Marketing Leader tier: they should be spending $3k–$15k/mo today on agency + tools. That's the budget Berlin redirects.

### 6. Decision authority
**Ask:** "If this looks like a fit, who needs to be involved to say yes?"

Founders should say "me." Marketing Leaders should have budget authority in their tier, even if there's a formal procurement step. Anyone else is a proxy — insert yourself into the real decision-maker's next conversation.

## Call Structure & Framework

### Pre-Call Prep (5 min)
- LinkedIn / company site review → pre-classify the cell
- Hypothesize trigger and current state
- Set internal agenda (explicit verbal agenda only for later-stage, multi-stakeholder calls)

### 1. Opening & Rapport (3–5 min)
- Thank them, confirm time
- Light rapport
- If they open with "Tell me about Berlin" or "What's the price," redirect:
  > "Happy to get to that — let me ask a few things first so the answer's relevant to your situation rather than generic. Fair?"

### 2. Discovery (12–15 min)
Run the 6 qualifying sections above, in roughly this order:
1. Archetype
2. Buyer persona + team context
3. Current state (DIY / freelancer / agency / in-house)
4. Trigger / why now
5. Budget / current spend
6. Decision authority

### 3. Positioning & Demo (8–10 min)
Lead with outcomes, not features. Anchor on what the prospect just told you.

**Core positioning line:**
> "You told me [X]. Here's what that looks like in Berlin: [dashboard conversation → Berlin proposes strategy → you approve → Berlin executes]. The thing you'd otherwise do with an agency or an in-house hire, Berlin does end-to-end, and you see every step."

**Three-pillar framing (Strategy / Execution / Tools):**
- **Strategy:** Berlin proposes a plan based on your business, ICP, and competitive landscape. You can accept, modify, or bring your own.
- **Execution:** Berlin writes, ships, updates, and monitors. Not "recommendations" — actual published output.
- **Tools:** All the underlying SEO/AEO tooling (rank tracking, crawl, entity analysis, AI-overview monitoring) is included, not a separate line item.

**Demo flow:**
1. Start at the dashboard conversation — show the prospect asking Berlin about *their* business.
2. Show Berlin producing a strategy artifact.
3. Show one concrete execution output (published page, internal linking change, schema update) tied to their pain point.
4. Close the loop: "So instead of briefing an agency and waiting two weeks, that just happened in the conversation."

Check comprehension every 2–3 minutes. If they're lost, simplify.

### 4. Closing (3–5 min)
See closing section below.

## Persona-Specific Approaches

### Cell 1 — SaaS × Founder ($499–$999)
- **Pain anchors:** Losing organic discovery to ChatGPT/Perplexity; no bandwidth; freelancer burn.
- **Lead with:** "Berlin is what you'd hire a fractional SEO lead to do, at a fraction of the cost, and without the onboarding."
- **Emphasize:** AI search visibility, time savings, no-hire execution.
- **Watch for:** SEO isn't top-3 priority; may stall. Create urgency around AEO/AI-overview decay.

### Cell 2 — SaaS × Marketing Leader ($2.5k–$5k)
- **Pain anchors:** Underperforming agency, slow reporting, no AI story from current provider.
- **Lead with:** "Berlin replaces the agency retainer with a platform that does the same work, faster, with full visibility."
- **Emphasize:** Transparency, speed-to-publish, AEO coverage, no-renewal lock-in.
- **Watch for:** Political lift of firing an agency. Offer a parallel-pilot path.

### Cell 3 — eCommerce × Founder ($499–$999)
- **Pain anchors:** PDP / category page scale; can't keep up; AI shopping surfaces.
- **Lead with:** "Every unoptimized product page is lost revenue. Berlin optimizes at SKU scale without you hand-editing."
- **Emphasize:** PDP automation, category structure, AI shopping visibility (ChatGPT Shopping, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity).
- **Watch for:** App-store mindset — they may expect $29/mo. Anchor on revenue recovered, not tool cost.

### Cell 4 — eCommerce × Marketing Leader ($2.5k–$5k)
- **Pain anchors:** Agency underperformance; enterprise tools (Bloomreach, Conductor) too heavy; AI shopping unknown.
- **Lead with:** "Berlin replaces the agency and covers what enterprise tools won't — actual execution across SKUs."
- **Emphasize:** Margin recovery, SKU-scale execution, AEO for shopping.
- **Watch for:** Entrenched contracts. Ask about renewal date early.

### Cell 5 — Lead Gen / Services × Founder ($499–$999)
- **Pain anchors:** SEO is existential; agency burn; want control + visibility.
- **Lead with:** "You've been burned by agencies. Berlin gives you the outcome without handing over the wheel."
- **Emphasize:** Transparency, conversion instrumentation, local/geo if relevant.
- **Watch for:** Skeptical buyer — lead with proof, not promise. Offer scoped proof-of-value.

### Cell 6 — Lead Gen / Services × Marketing Leader ($2.5k–$5k)
- **Pain anchors:** SEO is one line item among many; needs to be managed without eating their attention.
- **Lead with:** "Berlin makes SEO a channel you don't have to babysit — results, reports, no weekly agency check-ins."
- **Emphasize:** Reportability upward, time-back, predictability.
- **Watch for:** If SEO isn't prioritized internally, may not defend budget. Find the upward reporting angle.

## Common Objections & Responses

### Positioning objections (new — post-repositioning)

**"How is this different from hiring an SEO agency?"**
> "An agency gives you people who use tools to do SEO work. Berlin is the people, the tools, and the process, compressed into a platform you talk to. You get the same output — strategy, content, technical fixes, reporting — faster, fully transparent, and without the retainer structure."

**"Why wouldn't I just hire an in-house SEO lead?"**
> "A good in-house SEO lead is $120k–$180k fully loaded, takes three months to ramp, and still needs tools. Berlin replaces that hire's output on day one at [tier price]. When you eventually hire, Berlin makes them 3–4x more productive, so it's not wasted."

**"Can I still run my own strategy?"**
> "Yes, that's a supported path. You bring the strategy, Berlin executes and reports. Most customers start with Berlin proposing strategy and gradually take more of it on themselves — or stay in full auto, depending on their style."

**"What about the tools I already pay for — Ahrefs, SEMrush, Clearscope?"**
> "Those are fine — they're tools. Berlin includes the equivalent capabilities in the platform, so most customers drop them within 60 days. If you want to keep Ahrefs for its link index, you can; Berlin integrates with it."

**"Isn't this just a wrapper on GPT / Claude doing SEO?"**
> "No. Berlin has its own orchestration, its own ranking-signal stack, its own execution pipelines. LLMs are one component of many. The differentiator is that Berlin actually ships the work — writes, publishes, updates, monitors — not just generates suggestions."

**"How do I know the output quality matches what an agency would produce?"**
> "Fair question. Two answers: one, we'll show you production output from customers in your archetype on the call. Two, Berlin's output is reviewable — you approve before anything ships, or you don't, depending on how hands-on you want to be."

**"We have an agency contract until [date]."**
> "Good to know. Two paths: we can run Berlin in parallel for the last 60 days of your contract so you have a clean comparison before you renew, or we start the day your contract ends. Which fits better?"

### Standard objections

**"Pricing concerns"**
> "Compare it to what you're spending today. [Founder tier:] You're probably spending $500–$2,000 on tools and a freelancer. Berlin replaces all of that and goes further. [Marketing Leader tier:] You're likely on a $3k–$10k agency retainer. Berlin is the same spend or lower, with more output."

**"Can I just try it and cancel?"**
> "Month-to-month, no lock-in. What I recommend: we agree on 2–3 specific metrics upfront — say, AI-overview presence and organic conversions — set them up in week one, review at week four. That way you're measuring, not guessing."

**"Do you have a free trial?"**
> "No, because SEO results take time — a 7-day trial doesn't show real value. We're month-to-month instead. Let's set success metrics upfront and review at week four. If you're not seeing the trajectory, you cancel."

**"How do you ensure output quality / representative analysis?"**
> "We don't rely on single-shot LLM output. Berlin runs thousands of query variations, cross-checks against real ranking data and AI-overview presence, and every execution is reviewable before it ships if you want it to be."

**"We prefer human-only work."**
> "Berlin is human-directable at every step — you approve strategy, you approve output, you approve anything we publish. The AI does the heavy lifting; you keep judgment. Most customers settle into a mix where strategic calls are theirs and execution is Berlin's."

## Closing & Next Steps

### Standard close (preferred)
> "Based on what you've shared, I think [tier] fits. Here's what I recommend: we start [specific date]. Before then, you connect GSC, GA, and [CMS]. First week you'll see [specific output]. By week four, we review [agreed metrics]. Does [date] work, or is [alt date] better?"

### Proof-of-value close (use for skeptical but qualified)
Use when prospect is clearly in a target cell, has pain, but wants proof before committing.
> "Give me one page or one topic area and your biggest SEO pain around it. I'll have Berlin produce the strategy and one execution artifact within a week. We reconvene, you see real output on your actual site. If it's useful, we talk contract. If not, you got a free audit. Fair?"

**Rules:**
- Always book the follow-up before doing the work.
- One page / one topic / one artifact. No scope creep.
- Don't offer this more than 3× per week — it's expensive.

### Design-partner close (use for priority cells only)
> "You're exactly the profile we're learning from right now. I'd like to invite you into our design-partner program — [X]% off locked in, direct input on roadmap, weekly call for 8 weeks. In exchange we need real feedback and willingness to actually run SEO through Berlin. Interested?"

### What makes a strong close
- **Specific start date** — not "soon"
- **Clear prerequisites** — what they connect / send before kickoff
- **Binary scheduling** — "Tuesday or Wednesday?"
- **Agreed success metrics** — no ambiguity at week 4

### Weak closes to avoid
- "I'll send you an email"
- "Let me know when you're ready"
- "Sounds good, talk soon"
- Letting prospect defer to an internal meeting without you

## Pricing Confidence

### Tiers
- **Founder tier — $499–$999 / mo.** Cells 1, 3, 5. Self-serve-leaning with FDM onboarding.
- **Marketing Leader tier — $2,500–$5,000 / mo.** Cells 2, 4, 6. FDM-led, higher-touch.
- **Design-partner discount.** 30–50% off list, locked in, for priority cells only.

### If they push back on price
1. Don't discount casually — discount only via the design-partner lane.
2. Reframe to what they're replacing — agency retainer, tool stack, in-house hire.
3. Connect to a specific outcome they mentioned.
4. Confirm fit before negotiating — if they're not in the grid, price is a symptom, not the issue.

### Never say
- "We're still figuring out pricing"
- "We're early-stage so..."
- "It's expensive for us to run"
- Any statement that implies uncertainty

### Using price increases for urgency
Only if genuinely planned, and always paired with a CTA:
> "List is moving to $X next quarter. If you start before [date], you lock in today's rate. Want me to hold that?"

## Critical Mistakes to Avoid

### Positioning
- Don't pitch Berlin as a workflow builder, a "programmable infrastructure," or a tool you build on top of. That's the old positioning. Berlin is an **end-to-end platform** — strategy, execution, tools.
- Don't pitch USOL. It's internal.
- Don't position against Ahrefs / SEMrush as peers. Position against **agencies and in-house hires**.
- Don't say "we replace your tools" as the headline — say "we replace your agency" (tools come along for the ride).

### Discovery
- Don't skip cell placement. Every prospect must land in one of the 6 cells before you demo.
- Don't skip budget. Ask plainly: "What are you spending on SEO today, across everything?"
- Don't skip the trigger. No trigger = no close.
- Don't accept a proxy buyer. Insert yourself into the real decision-maker's conversation.

### Demo
- Don't over-explain architecture. The prospect cares about outcomes, not entity graphs or orchestration.
- Don't lead with features (80 signals, N integrations). Lead with outcomes tied to what they said in discovery.
- Don't volunteer incompleteness. If asked directly about a gap, be honest; don't unprompted.
- Don't peer-talk technology with technical prospects. Stay on business outcomes.

### Pricing
- Don't hedge. State the tier, connect to value, stop.
- Don't discount without the design-partner frame.
- Don't justify price with your costs ("it's expensive for us to run").

### Closing
- Don't close vaguely ("I'll send an email").
- Don't let prospects defer to internal meetings without you.
- Don't skip booking the next action on the current call.

## What Good Discovery Looks Like

**Phase 1 — Current state (4 min):** Business, role, SEO today (DIY / freelancer / agency / in-house).
**Phase 2 — Pain & trigger (4 min):** What hurts, what changed, why now.
**Phase 3 — Cell placement (3 min):** Confirm archetype, confirm buyer tier, map tool and spend landscape.
**Phase 4 — Decision mapping (2 min):** Who signs, what's the process, what's the timeline.
**Phase 5 — Success criteria (2 min):** What does "working" look like at 30 and 90 days.

### Red flags — when to disqualify
- Doesn't fit any of the 6 cells (marketplace, pure agency, media publisher, consumer-app-with-no-SEO, etc.)
- $0 current SEO spend with no trigger
- Proxy buyer with no path to the real decision-maker
- Unrealistic expectations ("#1 ranking in 2 weeks")
- Wants bespoke workflow building — that's the old positioning, and we're not that product anymore

## Learning Log

> **Note (2026-04-23):** Entries above were pruned during the repositioning from "programmable infrastructure for agencies" to "end-to-end platform replacing agencies." The entries below survived the cut because they generalize to the new motion. Entries flagged with an asterisk (*) were learned under the old positioning — sanity-check before applying, as the context has shifted.

- **Redirect opening questions.** When a prospect opens with "tell me about Berlin" or "what's the price," redirect with permission: "Happy to — let me ask a few things first so the answer's relevant." Works across personas.
- **Always ask about budget.** Don't skip because it feels awkward. "What are you spending on SEO today, across everything?" is the cleanest version.
- **Always ask 'why now?'.** Without a trigger, there's no close.
- **Don't volunteer incompleteness.** If asked directly about a gap, be honest — but don't unprompted. Prospects take offered uncertainty as reason to hesitate.
- **Insert yourself into the next conversation.** When prospects defer to an internal meeting, ask to join. Never let a proxy buyer carry your pitch into the decision-maker's office.
- **Comprehension checks during demo.** Every 2–3 minutes: "Does this map to how you'd use it?" Don't monologue.
- **Backlink honesty.*** Berlin's backlink automation is limited. If a prospect's pain is primarily backlinks, acknowledge briefly and pivot to what Berlin does strongly (on-page, internal linking, entity coverage, AEO). Don't over-probe on a gap.
- **Don't talk about our internal journey.*** Prospects don't care about agency→SaaS, LLM experiments, or rebuild history. Start with what Berlin does for them.
- **Educational lectures are a trap.*** Don't explain crawl budgets or LLM mechanics. Keep any explanation to 1–2 sentences, then pivot to outcome.
- **Peer conversation trap.*** With technical prospects, it's easy to stay in a tech discussion instead of talking business value. Resist.
- **Concrete timelines, not vague ones.*** Never say "compound effects" or "many variables." Say: "Month 1: foundation. Month 3: 20–30% improvement on [metric]."
- **State price, then connect to value.*** Don't hedge. "$X/mo. That replaces [what they're spending today] and delivers [outcome they mentioned]."

### Flagged for review (learned under old positioning, may not generalize)

- Agency-specific workflow probes (how do you onboard clients, how do you report, multi-client dashboards) — these were built for the old agency ICP. Skip unless the prospect is a Cell 6 services-firm marketer who genuinely reports upward.
- Founding Partner exclusivity language ("we're selecting 5 agencies") — the new equivalent exists (design-partner program) but the agency framing is dead.
- Tech-savvy agency owner / internal dev team sub-segments — those prospects self-selected under the old workflow-builder positioning. The new product doesn't target them.
