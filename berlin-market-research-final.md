# SEO, AEO, and GEO Market Research

> Research cut-off: July 24, 2026
> Scope: SEO software and services, AI-search visibility and optimization, competitive activity, and the labor-value pool relevant to Berlin.

***

## How to Read This Report

This market is real and growing, but its published size is unusually hard to measure. Research firms use inconsistent definitions: some reports labeled “SEO software” include social media, email, paid search, display, video, content marketing, and services. This report therefore separates:

* **Observed facts:** regulatory filings, completed transactions, disclosed revenue, and research with a stated sample and methodology.

* **Directional evidence:** vendor surveys, clickstream panels, and company-reported customer metrics.

* **Low-confidence forecasts:** syndicated market-size models whose full methodology and category boundaries are not public.

* **Berlin scenarios:** explicit assumptions for planning, not third-party market facts or forecasts.

No market figures in this report should be added together unless their scopes are demonstrably non-overlapping.

***

## 1. Executive Summary

The core thesis holds: search discovery is expanding from traditional search results into AI-generated answers, and marketing teams need to measure and improve visibility across both. The evidence does **not**, however, support presenting SEO software as a cleanly measured \$85–96 billion software market or adding an estimated SEO labor pool to it.

### Decision-ready conclusions

1. **Published market sizes are broad and low-confidence.** Precedence Research estimates a $96.42 billion “SEO software” market in 2026, but its taxonomy includes social media, email, paid search, display, video, and content marketing. Market Research Future estimates enterprise SEO platforms at $11.35 billion in 2025. These are best treated as outer market envelopes, not verified software revenue pools. ([Precedence Research](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/seo-software-market), [Market Research Future](https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/reports/enterprise-seo-platforms-market-41167))

2. **Observable commercial scale is substantial but far below the broad forecasts.** Semrush reported $443.6 million of 2025 revenue and $471.4 million of year-end ARR. It had approximately 108,000 paying customers, down from 117,000 a year earlier, while ARR per customer rose from $3,522 to $4,369. That combination points to low-end softness alongside successful upmarket and AI-product monetization. ([Semrush 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1831840/000162828026013259/semr-20251231.htm))

3. **AI-search exposure is already mass-market.** OpenAI reported more than 900 million weekly active ChatGPT users in February 2026. Google reported more than 2.5 billion monthly active users for AI Overviews and more than one billion for AI Mode in June 2026. These are platform-reported reach figures, not counts of commercially valuable search sessions, but they remove any doubt that AI answers are a mainstream discovery surface. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/scaling-ai-for-everyone/), [Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/))

4. **AI answers reduce click propensity on affected queries, but the aggregate traffic story is mixed.** Pew observed traditional-result clicks on 8% of Google visits with an AI summary versus 15% without one; only 1% clicked a source inside the summary. Ahrefs found AI Overviews correlated with a 58% lower position-one CTR in its December 2025 sample. Seer Interactive, however, observed AIO-query organic CTR rebound from 1.3% in December 2025 to 2.4% in February 2026, and Google says aggregate organic click volume is relatively stable. The right conclusion is not “SEO traffic disappears”; it is that rank alone no longer measures discovery value. ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/), [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update), [Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-search-driving-more-queries-higher-quality-clicks/))

5. **AI referrals are growing quickly from a small base and can be high-intent.** Ahrefs found AI traffic averaged only 0.25% of total site traffic across 81,947 sites in June 2025 despite 9.7x year-over-year growth. Adobe’s much larger U.S. retail dataset found AI-source traffic up 393% year over year in Q1 2026 and converting 42% better than non-AI traffic in March. Similarweb measured a 7.1% conversion rate for ChatGPT referrals across its April–May 2026 panel, close to paid search at 7.8%. These figures are not interchangeable; they show that sector, site, attribution method, and AI product design matter. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-traffic-increase/), [Adobe](https://business.adobe.com/blog/ai-traffic-surge-retail-sites-not-machine-readable), [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/marketing/geo/gen-ai-stats/))

6. **Capital and M\&A provide stronger category validation than syndicated forecasts.** Adobe completed its $1.87 billion acquisition of Semrush in April 2026. Sitecore acquired Scrunch in June 2026. Profound raised a $96 million Series C at a \$1 billion valuation in February 2026, while Peec AI, AirOps, daydream, and Gushwork also raised material rounds. The market is moving from standalone monitoring toward integrated insight, content, workflow, and execution. ([Adobe Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000112/adbe-20260529.htm), [Sitecore](https://www.sitecore.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2026/06/sitecore-acquires-scrunch-to-help-brands-influence-discovery--and-buying-decisions), [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/profound-raises-96m-series-c))

### Implication for Berlin

The most defensible opportunity is not “capture a percentage of an \$85 billion SEO software TAM.” It is to win a defined customer segment with a system that joins:

1. traditional SEO and AI-search measurement;
2. diagnosis and prioritization;
3. content and technical execution;
4. cross-engine monitoring; and
5. reporting tied to traffic, pipeline, and revenue.

Berlin’s SAM and SOM should be built from a validated ideal-customer profile, account counts, attainable annual contract value, and delivery capacity. Until those inputs are set, any nine-figure SOM is a scenario rather than a market fact.

### 2.2 What can safely be said about TAM

* The **broad economic pool** around organic visibility includes software, agencies, consultants, internal labor, content production, technical implementation, digital PR, and adjacent marketing tools. It is plausibly worth tens of billions of dollars, but there is no reliable public dataset that cleanly de-duplicates those components.

* The **pure software pool** is materially smaller than the broad $96.42 billion headline. Publicly observable Semrush revenue alone is $443.6 million, while most other major vendors are private and do not publish audited revenue.

* The **GEO/AEO category is early and fast-changing**. Multiple forecasts cluster around $1–1.5 billion in 2025–2026 and ~$17 billion in 2034, but those estimates should not be used as precise investment-case inputs.

* SEO, AEO, and GEO overlap. GEO revenue added by an incumbent SEO platform may already sit inside both market definitions, so adding the two estimates would double-count spend.

**Recommended external wording:** “Berlin operates across a large, established organic-visibility market and a rapidly forming AI-search optimization category. Published estimates vary by more than an order of magnitude because definitions mix software and services.”

### 2.4 Berlin’s SAM

SAM cannot be inferred by applying a percentage to a syndicated market forecast. It must be defined by who Berlin can serve with its current product and delivery model.

The required inputs are:

`SAM = target accounts × annual contract value × product-fit rate`

The model should segment at minimum by:

* customer type: agency, in-house mid-market, or enterprise;

* geography and language;

* site or portfolio complexity;

* traditional SEO and AI-search maturity;

* required integrations and governance;

* self-serve, platform, hybrid, or managed-service delivery;

* credible annual contract value.

The previous \$11.5–50 billion SAM range is removed because it mixed incompatible definitions and did not establish how many buyers fit Berlin’s target profile.

### 2.5 Berlin’s SOM: explicit planning scenarios

The following are **illustrative revenue scenarios**, not forecasts:

| Scenario                | Customers | Average annual contract value | Annual recurring revenue |
| ----------------------- | --------: | ----------------------------: | -----------------------: |
| Focused wedge           |       500 |                      \$24,000 |                    \$12M |
| Scaled mid-market       |     1,500 |                      \$36,000 |                    \$54M |
| Category-scale platform |     5,000 |                      \$60,000 |                   \$300M |

These scenarios become decision-grade only after validating target-account counts, win rates, sales-cycle length, gross retention, expansion, implementation capacity, and gross margin. A “2% of market” shortcut is not a credible SOM methodology.

## 3. Key Market Trends

### Trend 1: AI search is a global discovery surface

| Surface             | Latest first-party reach disclosed by research cut-off | Scope                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------- | -----------------------------------------------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ChatGPT             |                     More than 900M weekly active users | Global ChatGPT usage, February 2026. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/scaling-ai-for-everyone/))                              |
| Google AI Overviews |                    More than 2.5B monthly active users | Google-reported, June 2026. ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/)) |
| Google AI Mode      |                      More than 1B monthly active users | Google-reported, June 2026. ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/)) |

These figures use different definitions and cannot be compared as market share. They do establish that optimizing only for traditional blue links leaves a material discovery surface unmeasured.

### Trend 2: Strategy has changed faster than operations

Semrush surveyed 481 marketers, SEO professionals, business owners, and related roles in 2026:

* 85% said AI had changed their search strategy;

* only 22% said SEO and AI search were fully integrated across strategy, execution, and reporting;

* only 9% said they could measure all the metrics that matter;

* 40% still relied on manual ChatGPT checks as their main tracking method.

The sample is disclosed, but Semrush sells into this category, so the figures are directional rather than neutral market incidence. They nevertheless identify a credible operational gap between awareness and repeatable execution. ([Semrush study and methodology](https://www.semrush.com/blog/the-operational-gap-ai-seo-study/))

Conductor’s survey of more than 250 U.S. enterprise digital leaders found 94% planned to increase AEO investment in 2026. It is also a vendor-sponsored survey and its respondents reported unusually high AEO maturity, so it is best read as evidence of **enterprise budget intent**, not adoption across the whole market. ([Conductor report](https://www.conductor.com/academy/state-of-aeo-geo-report/))

The commercial implication is a measurement change: impressions, citations, brand mentions, sentiment, assisted conversions, and downstream demand must sit beside rankings and last-click organic sessions.

### Trend 4: AI referral traffic is small, volatile, and sometimes high-intent

* **Broad-web baseline:** Ahrefs’ June 2025 analysis of 81,947 sites found AI traffic had grown about 9.7x year over year but averaged only 0.25% of total site traffic. Google still sent 210x more traffic than the three largest AI referrers combined. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-traffic-increase/))

* **Retail:** Adobe analyzed more than one trillion visits to U.S. retail sites. AI-source traffic rose 393% year over year in Q1 2026; in March, it converted 42% better than non-AI traffic, with 12% higher engagement and 48% longer time on site. These are retail-specific results, not universal conversion benchmarks. ([Adobe](https://business.adobe.com/blog/ai-traffic-surge-retail-sites-not-machine-readable))

* **Cross-site clickstream panel:** Similarweb reported a 7.1% ChatGPT-referral conversion rate in April–May 2026, close to paid search at 7.8%. A ChatGPT link-format change on May 7 also produced a 157.7% week-over-week referral increase in its tracked panel, illustrating how product changes can abruptly alter attribution and traffic. ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/marketing/geo/gen-ai-stats/))

The previous universal claims of 14.2% conversion, 23x higher conversion, and 6–27x B2B conversion are removed. The available evidence supports a narrower conclusion: **AI referrals can be disproportionately valuable for some sectors and sites, but volume and conversion vary too much for one cross-market multiplier.**

### Trend 5: Buyers are replacing and rationalizing search tooling

The 2025 MarTech Replacement Survey had 207 respondents, of whom 154 had replaced an application in the previous year. SEO tools were the most-replaced category; 37.1% cited AI capabilities as important in replacement decisions, while 43.8% cited cost reduction. This supports replacement pressure, but not the previous unsupported claim that every agency uses four to six tools or that consolidation saves 40–60%. ([Search Engine Land summary and methodology](https://searchengineland.com/seo-tools-martech-replacements-473213))

In a separate 2025 study of 233 Fortune 500 martech decision-makers, 47% cited stack complexity and system/data integration as key blockers to value. This strengthens the case for workflow integration and measurable business outcomes over another isolated dashboard. ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/rewiring-martech-from-cost-center-to-growth-engine))

### Trend 6: The product category is moving from monitoring to action

Competitive product moves and acquisitions point in the same direction:

* Profound expanded from AI visibility monitoring into agents and orchestration.

* AirOps describes an end-to-end system spanning insight, prioritization, creation, and refresh.

* Sitecore acquired Scrunch to connect AI-search insight to content-management execution.

* Adobe combined Semrush visibility data with its content, experience, and customer-engagement stack.

* daydream sells an AI-native service combining SEO agents with a human growth lead.

* [Maintouch](https://maintouch.com/) and [Gushwork](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/25/gushwork-bets-on-ai-search-for-customer-leads-and-early-results-are-emerging/) position software or agents as alternatives to labor-heavy agency workflows.

The opportunity is therefore not an empty market. It is a contested transition from **systems of record** to **systems of action**.

### Trend 7: Owned data and machine readability matter, but the cookie rationale changed

First-party data, proprietary crawling, structured content, and machine-readable product or company information can create data-quality and unit-cost advantages. Adobe’s 2026 retail analysis found an average machine-readability score of 66% for product pages in its checker, implying material content-access gaps.

The earlier report incorrectly cited the universal phase-out of third-party cookies as a continuing driver. In April 2025, Google said Chrome would maintain its existing user-choice approach and would not launch a new standalone third-party-cookie prompt. The case for owned data remains strong, but it rests on reliability, differentiation, privacy resilience, and economics—not a completed Chrome cookie deprecation. ([Google Privacy Sandbox update](https://privacysandbox.google.com/blog/privacy-sandbox-next-steps))

## 4. The AEO/GEO Opportunity

### 4.1 Definitions

* **SEO:** improving discoverability and performance in conventional search results.

* **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization):** making content eligible, clear, and authoritative for direct-answer surfaces, including snippets and AI-generated answers.

* **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization):** improving how a brand, entity, product, or source is represented, cited, and recommended by generative search and assistant systems.

* **AI-search visibility:** the broader measurable outcome across mentions, citations, sentiment, accuracy, source influence, referral traffic, and business results.

The terms overlap and the market has not standardized them. For customers, the durable category is likely **search and brand visibility across human- and AI-mediated discovery**, not a permanent split between SEO, AEO, and GEO.

### 4.2 Consumer behavior

McKinsey’s August 2025 survey of a representative U.S. panel of 1,927 consumers found:

* 50% intentionally sought out AI-powered search;

* 44% of AI-search users called it their primary and preferred source of insight, compared with 31% for traditional search;

* 40–55% of consumers in several major sectors used AI search in purchase decisions.

McKinsey also projected that $750 billion in U.S. revenue could flow through AI-powered search by 2028 and that unprepared brands could see 20–50% traditional-search traffic declines. Those last two figures are **consulting projections**, not observed outcomes. ([McKinsey AI Discovery research](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/new-front-door-to-the-internet-winning-in-the-age-of-ai-search))

McKinsey’s 2026 five-country survey of 4,863 consumers found 28% of Gen Z respondents used generative AI for shopping versus 16% of baby boomers, while 60% of Gen Z regularly used AI Overviews versus 29% of baby boomers. Adoption is broadening, but it remains segmented by age, use case, geography, and trust. ([McKinsey State of the Consumer 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/state-of-consumer))

### 4.3 A defensible measurement framework

Berlin should measure a portfolio of outcomes rather than one “GEO rank”:

1. **Presence:** share of relevant prompts or answers in which the brand appears.
2. **Citation share:** share and position of cited sources, including owned and third-party pages.
3. **Representation quality:** factual accuracy, attribute coverage, positioning, and sentiment.
4. **Competitive share of voice:** presence relative to named competitors by topic, market, and engine.
5. **Technical eligibility:** crawl access, rendering, indexability, structured data, freshness, and machine readability.
6. **Referral and assisted demand:** AI-referred sessions, engaged visits, conversions, branded search lift, and self-reported attribution.
7. **Traditional search performance:** rankings, impressions, clicks, conversions, and revenue.
8. **Business outcomes:** qualified pipeline, customer acquisition cost, revenue, retention, and margin.

Because engines, prompts, locations, models, and answer formats change, all AI-visibility measurements should document sampling frequency, prompt set, personalization state, geography, and model version.

### 4.4 Strategic opportunity

The evidence supports four product requirements:

* **SEO and GEO should share one workflow.** Traditional organic authority, crawlability, content quality, and third-party reputation still influence AI discovery.

* **Multi-engine coverage is essential.** ChatGPT, Google AI surfaces, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot, and emerging agents differ in sources and behavior.

* **Off-site influence matters.** AI answers often draw on publishers, reviews, communities, affiliates, and other third-party sources, so optimizing only the owned website is insufficient.

* **Action and business measurement are the scarce layers.** Monitoring is increasingly available from incumbents and startups. Prioritizing work, executing safely, and tying it to commercial outcomes remain harder.

### 5.3 Competitive conclusions

1. **Monitoring alone is becoming crowded.** Incumbents and specialist startups can all track prompts, mentions, citations, sources, and sentiment.
2. **The action layer is the battleground.** Competitors are adding content creation, prioritization, technical recommendations, agents, and workflow orchestration.
3. **Incumbent distribution is a serious advantage.** Adobe/Semrush and Sitecore/Scrunch can cross-sell AI visibility into established content and experience budgets.
4. **Services are not disappearing.** daydream’s funding and Gushwork’s hybrid history show that customers still pay for execution and accountability, especially while the discipline is immature.
5. **Berlin must choose a wedge.** Agencies, mid-market teams, and enterprises require different governance, integrations, pricing, and service levels. “End-to-end for everyone” is not a go-to-market segment.

## 6. Labor Value and Pricing Lens

### 6.1 Labor is not TAM

Internal employee salaries are a customer cost pool, not vendor revenue. They should not be added to SEO software or GEO services market estimates. The earlier \$15–45 billion “labor-addressable TAM” is removed because:

* the cited count of 207,000 SEO professionals originated from an old LinkedIn-title search;

* title counts omit people who perform SEO without an SEO title and may double-count or misclassify others;

* multiplying a workforce estimate by salary does not establish software-replaceable spend;

* software typically augments a team rather than replacing every task or role.

Labor is still useful for setting pricing ceilings and modeling customer ROI.

### 6.2 Current salary anchors

| Benchmark                  |            Value | Method and limitation                                                                                                                                                       |

### 6.3 Pricing scenarios against one U.S. specialist salary

| Subscription  | Annual price | Share of \$70,360 base salary |
| ------------- | -----------: | ----------------------------: |
| \$2,000/month |     \$24,000 |                           34% |
| \$3,000/month |     \$36,000 |                           51% |
| \$5,000/month |     \$60,000 |                           85% |

This comparison excludes benefits, payroll taxes, management, implementation, services, and the value of specialist judgment. It also does not assume a full role can be replaced. It simply shows why an execution platform that reliably saves material capacity or produces incremental gross profit can support pricing above a conventional per-seat SEO tool.

### 6.4 Recommended customer ROI model

Use a customer-level model rather than a global labor TAM:

`Annual value = capacity saved + avoided external spend + incremental gross profit`

Where:

* `capacity saved = verified hours saved × loaded hourly cost`;

* `avoided external spend = tools, contractors, or agency scope actually retired`;

* `incremental gross profit = attributable incremental revenue × gross margin`;

* ROI should be measured after subscription, implementation, and internal operating costs.

The key commercial question is not “how many SEO salaries exist?” It is “for which repeatable workflows can Berlin prove enough time, cost, or revenue impact to justify its contract value?”
