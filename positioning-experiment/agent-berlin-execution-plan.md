## 0 · The plan in one paragraph

**Two channels, one target: land 3 ICP-fit customers at the $2k floor in 8 weeks.** The **primary** channel is **warm founder-to-founder referral** — the only motion that has ever closed a deal here. The **secondary** (insurance) channel is **personalized wedge-finding cold** — where the *finding* is the personalization, fixing why the first un-personalized cold attempt failed. The **AEO Visibility audit is a sales artifact inside those conversations and the free wedge demo — not the engine, and not a paid revenue rung during the sprint.** Both channels convert into a **managed relationship priced on a coverage dial** (Co-Pilot ↔ Fully-Run). We **hunt one ICP** (founder-operator scaleups, 1–10M); the **vertical** experiment runs on the *cold* channel (which we can point), while the *warm* channel takes ICP-fit founders as they come (vertical opportunistic). Everything off the line — 10–50M inbound, the consult, no-team SMBs — is taken opportunistically for cash and **tagged out of the experiment.** Beyond the sprint we drive toward a defined PMF through three gated stages.

> **Why 3 is the number that governs everything:** it is the single target that unlocks **four things at once** — (a) runway **cash**, (b) the **signal that justifies the loan** (8 weeks → ~10 months), (c) the **dedicated-FDM hire** that relieves the founder's time bottleneck, and (d) **n=3 replication** of the ICP hypothesis. Subordinate every sprint decision to it.

## 1 · The ICP classification (three *classification* axes — non-vertical)

| Axis | Why it matters to *our* economics |

| **In-house execution capacity** | Has a 1–3 person team → Co-Pilot → ~60% margin. No team → Fully-Run → we eat the labor → bleed pre-product. |
| **Channel incumbency** | AEO-greenfield = no incumbent, no testimonials needed. SEO rip-and-replace = slow, reference-hungry knife fight. |

These yield three ICPs:

| ICP | Profile | Role | Land motion / positioning |

## 2 · The experiment design (three *experiment* axes → one live variable)

We named axes we *could* vary: **vertical × revenue band × outreach angle × channel**. Varying more than one at a time = a confounded cube and we'd narrow on the wrong thing. The discipline: **all but one are decided, so they are constants, not variables.**

| Experiment axis | Status | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Channel** | **CONSTANT split** | Warm referral = primary; wedge-finding cold = insurance secondary | Warm is the only proven close; cold is fixable insurance to reach 3 (see §0.5) |
| **Outreach angle** | **CONSTANT** | The **wedge finding** is the default touch; the audit is the *artifact*, not the offer; consult is the per-deal exception | The finding is on-wedge + solo-scalable; the consult is anti-leverage (see §5) |
| **Revenue band** | **CONSTANT** | Hunt 1–10M; 10–50M is opportunistic/inbound | 1–10M is the proven, fast, founder-led reallocation buyer |
| **Vertical** | **LIVE VARIABLE** *(on the cold channel)* | The one thing we actively vary — but only where we can point it | It's where the moat forms (§6) and the only clean signal a solo founder can read |

**Where the vertical read actually lives.** The **warm channel can't control vertical** (you take the ICP-fit founders your network hands you → vertical **opportunistic**, banked for cash). So the **clean vertical experiment runs on the *cold* channel**, which we *can* point: push the *identical* wedge-finding cold motion into 2–3 candidate verticals for a fixed window, holding band + angle constant. The difference in response **is** the signal. "Few weeks then switch" is legitimate **only when the switch is along the vertical axis.**

**Attribution (Close CRM via the dogfu pipeline):** stamp every lead with five fields — `vertical`, `band`, `angle`, `channel`, `experiment_vs_opportunistic`.

**The read:** *among experiment leads only (channel = cold, band = 1–10M, angle = wedge finding), which vertical has the best meeting-set rate and meeting→managed conversion?* Everything tagged `opportunistic` — **all warm-channel closes included** — is banked as cash but **excluded from the vertical read.** This separation is the whole game: it lets us take any revenue that walks in (and the warm channel *is* our main revenue in the sprint) without polluting the one signal we pay to read.

## 3 · The front-door: the wedge finding, with the audit as artifact

**What opens the conversation is the *wedge finding*, not an audit offer.** The audit is the **artifact you hand over** to make the finding concrete and the **free wedge demo** — it is *not* the thing you sell at the top of the funnel, and **its paid rung is shelved for the 8-week sprint** (an unproven cold conversion we can't afford to test on the survival clock). **Price is set by each rung's job in the funnel — not by the audit's ~$20–30 production cost.**

| Rung | What it is | Price | Job · sprint status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wedge finding / free teaser** | The specific finding (*"AI names your competitors, not you — here's the source"*) + a one-page snapshot of where you show up (and don't) in AI answers | **$0** (the touch itself) | **ACTIVE.** Opens warm *and* cold conversations; the wedge demo ("your agency can't see this — proof attached") |
| **Paid deep audit** | Comprehensive version; **FDM adds human depth** | **~$500–1,500** | **PARKED for the sprint.** A post-sprint qualifier/commitment device; do not chase it for cash now |
| **Managed retainer** | The coverage-dial relationship (§4) — **the real target of the sprint** | **$2k (Co-Pilot) / $4k (Fully-Run)** floors | **ACTIVE.** The recurring business; price every new deal at the floor (Customer 2 was under-priced at $799) |

**Hard rule — sequence human labor behind intent.** The free finding earns the meeting; comprehensive FDM hours go only behind real intent (a warm-qualified or clearly-engaged lead). Pouring human work onto unqualified cold leads rebuilds the Fully-Run margin trap at the top of the funnel.

> **Open question (discuss next):** do we productize *only* the audit and keep the managed offering as a scoped dial, or productize the managed offering too? See §7.

## 4 · The coverage dial (Co-Pilot ↔ Fully-Run)

One offer, one dial — the customer never picks a SKU. The dial moves *who executes*, and with it the margin profile.

`Left ── they run it (with our tool) ──── Co-Pilot (shared) ──── we run it all ── Right`
`high margin · product signal                                  high load · service signal`

Today's live notches: **Co-Pilot** (their team executes → ~60% margin) and **Fully-Run** (we execute end-to-end). The far-left **Guided / product-only** notch stays in the model as the end-state the cannibalization line drives toward, but isn't sold until the product can self-run.

**Fully-Run is a strategic engine, not a trap to avoid.** Even though it doesn't expose the product to the end user, our FDMs executing the work is:
- the **fastest product-feedback loop** (gaps surface in real use; the execution ledger fills with the exact backlog the product is built to absorb),
- the **case-study factory** (we control the outcome → a referenceable win), and
- **higher ACV.**

That's three of the four things we most lack pre-PMF, from one motion. The trap was only ever **subsidized Fully-Run for the wrong customer** (the $299 no-team clinic). At full freight for a paying scaleup that wants hands-off, Fully-Run is customer-funded product development.

**Operating call:** hunt **Co-Pilot for cash**, and **deliberately keep 1–2 full-price Fully-Run accounts as the R&D-and-proof engine.** Don't let them outnumber Co-Pilot (labor-heavy, one person). Tension held consciously, not resolved: *Co-Pilot = margin per unit of our effort today; Fully-Run = product progress + proof + ACV.*

## 5 · The consult — a deal-shape, not an engine

The AEO consult is **kept in the toolkit** but is **not an experiment.** Deploy it only when a specific deal's economics justify it — large ACV, a 10–50M logo worth having, a buyer who only moves high-touch.

- **Why not an engine:** it sells/delivers only on the founder, so it consumes build time and doesn't compound — anti-leverage for one person racing a product window.
- **Guardrails when deployed:** (1) substrate = our product, somewhere we control — be willing to lose the deal that demands "install into our stack"; (2) fixed scope, designed to **end on the managed dial**, not renew as advisory; (3) every install must feed reusable backlog.
- **Attribution:** always tag `opportunistic` so it never contaminates the vertical signal.

## 6 · Verticalization — concentrate, because the moat forms there

Pick **one vertical** and concentrate the audit + managed motion there. Not mainly for sharper messaging (though it helps) — because **the AEO source map** (which Reddit threads, review sites, comparison pages, forums and UGC the LLMs actually cite for a category) **is reusable across every customer in that vertical.** Map it once → every later audit is near-free to produce, every retainer's targets are pre-known, every win is a portable reference for the next identical buyer. **The asset compounds per vertical, not per customer** — the difference between linear grind and a flywheel for a lean team.

Anchor: **B2B SaaS / marketplaces** (where ICP 1 clusters and AI-search anxiety runs hottest); narrow one notch further into a category where "buyers ask AI for a recommendation" is *visibly already happening* and the sources are rich/mappable, so the first source map is deep, not broad. **Hunt one vertical narrow; serve any inbound wide.**

## 7 · The staged path to PMF

**PMF for this product-funded-by-service hybrid = three things true at once:**
1. a **repeatable** cold→audit→managed motion that closes at the floor without reinventing the pitch each time;
2. delivery **contribution ≥60%**;
3. the **product absorbing the execution layer** — founder-hours-per-account *falling* as accounts are added (the cannibalization line moving).

*Without #3 it's a profitable services business, not a product company — a legitimate choice, but name it.*

| Stage | Goal | Illustrative gate | Asset banked | Kill / pivot signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Repeatable LAND** (~8–10 wks) | Prove the audit→Co-Pilot motion converts in one vertical | Sell/deliver ~8–12 audits → convert **≥3 to managed at the $2k floor** within 8 weeks | Vertical source-map v1 + first repeatable pitch | Audits don't sell cold, or sell but won't convert (audit is a curiosity, not a wedge) |
| **S2 — Repeatable EXPAND + margin holds** | Prove economics + that we're not trading hours for dollars | Contribution **≥60%**; **≥1 land-and-expand** event; **founder-hours/account trending down** | Proof / case studies (esp. from the 1–2 Fully-Run accounts) | Margin only holds via founder over-delivery, or hours/account stay flat (product not absorbing → we're an agency) |
| **S3 — PMF** | The motion runs without the founder being the load-bearing element | Predictable close rate on audit→managed; margin holding; adding a customer no longer adds proportional founder time | A real decision: scale bootstrapped **or** raise | — |

Don't make the scale-vs-raise decision before S3; that would be raising on vibes.

## 8 · Differentiation — standing out in a saturated landscape

The landscape is brutally competitive and **almost every agency already offers a free or paid audit.** So the audit alone cannot be the differentiator — a bigger free thing just makes us *legible as "one of those."* You don't out-shout a red ocean; you **out-narrow + out-specific + on-wedge.** Test for any idea: *what can we credibly do or say that the 1,000 agencies structurally cannot?*

**Keep three jobs separate — they're not interchangeable:**

| Job | What it is | Our move |
|---|---|---|
| **Message** | What we actually say in the touch | Lead with the **wedge finding, delivered before they ask** — *"I asked ChatGPT/Perplexity to recommend a tool in your category; it named these competitors, not you, and here's the source it trusted."* Intelligence, not an offer. The audit is the attachment, not the hook. |
| **Proof** | Why they believe we can do it | The **open-source crawler** (better than ScreamingFrog) + the **Claude/Codex skills package** = credibility infra signaling *builder, not marketer*. The reason the hook is believable — not the hook itself. |
| **Motion** | How we reach them | Cold outreach, build-in-public, OSS distribution, (webinars). |

**Four lenses to score any differentiation move (priority order for our constraints):**

1. **Compounds vs evaporates** — favor assets (crawler, skills, a reusable per-vertical finding engine) over **time-for-attention trades** (webinar, consult) that burn the scarce founder and leave nothing behind.
2. **Demonstrates the wedge vs decorates** — on-thesis ("we see the sources LLMs cite") beats generic ("AI-native").
3. **Attracts ICP-1 specifically vs a generic crowd** — OSS + build-in-public draws technical founders; broad webinars pull low-intent crowds and competitors.
4. **Copyable by an agency or not** — our most defensible, authentic edge is that **we are a builder and they are marketers cosplaying AI-native.** Lead from the one axis they can't cross.

**Two bigger levers:**

- **Niche depth IS differentiation** (same problem as §6) — "the AEO person for *[one vertical]*" beats "an AI SEO agency"; a hyper-specific finding beats a generic audit.
- **Cold vs distribution-led motion** — the crawler/skills/build-in-public are really *distribution* plays that make later outreach *warm*. Decide which is the **engine** (near-term cash) and which is the **flywheel** (compounding warmth underneath).

> **The conclusion:** compete on the **finding** + the **identity (builder, not marketer)** + the **niche** — **not on the audit.** Crawler & skills = proof/distribution to invest in. Webinar & consult = the two to be most skeptical of (burn the founder, don't compound).
