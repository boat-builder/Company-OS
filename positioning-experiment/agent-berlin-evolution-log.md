# Agent Berlin — Strategy Evolution Log

*A succinct record of how the operating model was reasoned out, in the order it happened. Each step shows where we were, the turn, and where we landed. Use this to resume the thread.*

**The question we started from:** which direction to build? The SaaS/tool built so far had no moat and a low revenue ceiling.

---

1. **Three candidate directions on the table.** (a) Product/agent that runs itself for non-technical customers; (b) FDE consultancy that installs agentic systems inside bigger marketing teams; (c) FDM+FDE consultancy that also *runs* the marketing with agents + our skills. Concern running through all of it: moat + revenue ceiling.

2. **Adjacency check → collapsed back to the three.** Surveyed seven adjacent plays (infra-for-agencies, vertical wedge, productized fixed-scope, white-label/channel, embedded agent, outcome pricing, category engine). Conclusion: all are *extensions* of the three, not new foundations. Kept the three.

3. **Found the real axis underneath.** The variable wasn't "how automated" — it was *where the operating capability permanently lives, and therefore what you actually sell.* Three foundations: **Product** (sell the engine), **Enablement/FDE** (sell the install), **Managed** (sell the outcome). Automation-vs-human is a cost lever cutting across all three — never a fourth direction.

4. **Spine + focus chosen.** Product is the spine but not yet stable enough to sell standalone to any segment. Decision: run Enablement + Managed to see which sticks; both feed the product. Rejected: running them as two separate stories / two domains.

5. **"Merge" proposed → corrected to the real shape.** Idea: blur FDE/FDM into one elastic "FDM." Correction: it isn't a merge — classic enablement (leave the customer independent of us) drops out, and *should*, because product is the spine. Real shape: **managed service with a setup front-door and a self-serve ceiling — one business, one dial.** The dial is the PMF instrument: let customers reveal where demand clusters instead of pre-betting on a model.

6. **The operating discipline.** The blur is a go-to-market *wrapper*, not an org model. Internally keep an execution ledger (product-agentic vs. human-executed vs. setup-vs-ongoing). The hard line: **every human task is product backlog — the day we stop cannibalizing human work into the product, we're an agency again.** Blur the title, not the skill pools. → *Positioning doc produced.*

7. **Pricing — the "components" worry.** Realized "setup" is *continuous* (models/tooling keep evolving) but ~80–90% front-loaded, with a thin perpetual tail. Worry: a three-part price (setup + platform + metered) is hard to communicate cold. Resolution: the problem isn't the number of components, it's the number of *decisions forced on the buyer*; present **one decision** at the close. Dropped the "80% off setup" mechanic — it manufactures a phantom anchor and invites suspicion.

8. **Pricing — the unit.** Proposed a base fee + a package of hours, with setup absorbed in the early weeks. Correction: **don't sell hours.** Hours punish you for succeeding (cannibalization is designed to make them vanish) and reduce you to a body shop managed by timesheet. **Sell coverage/scope; cost it in hours internally.** Then efficiency gains become retention/expansion assets, not billing problems.

9. **Coverage vs. capability split.** Separated two fused axes: **Capability** = what we *can* do (the differentiated intelligence layer — the thing traditional SEO teams can't offer; show it in full to *everyone*; it's the wedge) vs. **Coverage** = how much we run (the dial). **Tier by coverage, not capability.** Always-on intelligence layer (identical in every tier) + execution layer (what the dial actually moves). Three tiers: **Guided / Co-Pilot / Fully-Run.** *(Status note, 2026-06-22: only **Co-Pilot** and **Fully-Run** are sold today. **Guided / product-only is NOT yet sold** — the product isn't stable enough to hand off for self-run. Guided stays in the model as the target end-state the cannibalization line drives toward; see the price grid.)*

10. **Scale as a second axis.** Effort differs massively by company size *within* a tier (10-page single-product vs. 10k-SKU competitive ecom). Rejected sub-tiers — they rebuild the matrix and make customers shop their own scale, which they systematically underestimate. **Coverage = decision axis (customer picks); Scale = sizing axis (we scope).** Measure scale in *customer-surface units* (product lines, markets, languages, SKU bands, source ecosystems), never hours. Scale also splits the motion: small surface = menu-priced fast quote; large surface = must-be-scoped.

11. **Mechanics + the experiment frame.** Internally a **9-cell grid** (coverage × scale) as the pricing reference; externally one coverage choice + one scoping call, with **no prices on the website** — because pre-PMF the pricing call is the single richest data source, and a page price throws it away. Key reframe that kills the combinatorial explosion: **scale is costing (off the experiment table), coverage is *observed* not varied, segment is the only axis we actively vary.** So it isn't 54 cells to test — it's *one* segment experiment, with coverage watched and scale priced underneath. Run **2–3 *sharp* segment bets (≥1 a vertical, because the moat forms there fastest), hunt narrow / serve wide, and pre-commit a signal threshold + kill date per segment.** Resist go-wide-then-dial-down — wide-and-generic gives confounded signal you'll narrow on by mistake.

## Continuation — ICP & saleable surface (2026-06-22)

*Picks up the thread above. Same format: where we were, the turn, where we landed.*

12. **Product not ready → the dial has only two live notches.** The self-run **Guided / product-only** tier can't be sold yet — the product isn't stable enough to hand a customer to run themselves. Decision: **sell only Co-Pilot and Fully-Run today;** keep Guided in every doc as the target end-state the cannibalization line drives toward, never quote it. (Marked throughout the price grid.)

13. **ICP reframed from "which title" to "which person clears every constraint at once."** The real question wasn't founder-SMB *vs* marketing-team *vs* CMO as separate options — it was finding the one profile that simultaneously: has authority for a $2k+/mo recurring decision, **already spends on SEO/AEO** (so we're a *reallocation*, not a new-budget fight), has a team that can carry execution, and is reachable on **thesis + founder-led content** rather than testimonials (which we don't have). That collapses to one person: **the founder (or founder-delegated growth/marketing lead) of a fast-growing B2B / B2B2C SaaS or marketplace (~$20k–$150k+ MRR, climbing) that already has a 1–3 person marketing+SEO function.** → *ICP summary doc produced (`agent-berlin-icp.md`).*

14. **The two existing customers read as a natural experiment — with a caveat.** Clinic: Fully-Run (we did all execution covertly), no team, $299/mo, **unprofitable**. Job platform: Co-Pilot (their SEO person executes), has a team, $799/mo, **workable**; founder is the buyer; grew 20k→100k MRR in a year; passed on a $4k agency they *liked*. The honest read: n=2 with many confounded variables **proves nothing on its own.** But there's a *structural* reason underneath it that doesn't depend on the sample: **pre-product, Fully-Run makes us supply the execution labor at our cost; Co-Pilot lets the customer's team supply it at theirs.** So **Co-Pilot is the profitable wedge today; Fully-Run is the margin trap until the product carries the work** — *and also* the strategic growth tier, because Fully-Run generates the human-task backlog the product is built to absorb. Treat "Co-Pilot is more profitable per unit of our effort now" as a hypothesis to confirm on the next 3–4 deals, not a settled fact.

15. **Pricing floors set against delivery cost.** The earlier $500-margin worry was a *mislabel*, not a model failure: it was a Fully-Run delivery cost (~$1.5k) charged at a Co-Pilot price ($2k). Corrected: **~$2k/mo floor at the "your team owns execution" (Co-Pilot) end, ~$4k/mo floor at the "we run everything" (Fully-Run) end,** scaled by surface — both ~60% contribution if cost discipline holds (Co-Pilot <$1k, Fully-Run ~$1.5k). The $4k agency the job platform turned down confirms the market tolerates this range; we are the credible value option beneath it. **We under-priced #2 at $799** — price every *new* deal at the floor. Caveat: at this ACV it's a low-ACV grind — a real business only via **land-and-expand** (ride the customer's growth up the dial and across surface); flat $2k forever is a lifestyle business, not a venture one.

16. **One external offer, not two buckets — reaffirms "one business, one dial."** Considered splitting Co-Pilot / Fully-Run into two named external SKUs. Rejected, for the same reason sub-tiers were rejected for scale: two SKUs force the buyer to self-classify before they understand the work, anchor a "why is it double?" price comparison that exposes cost structure, and turn the natural drift between modes into friction. Decisive point: **how much the customer's team owns is our biggest cost/margin lever — keep it a live dial in the scoping conversation, not a fixed price card.** So: **one offer; coverage and scale both internal dials; "Co-Pilot / Fully-Run" survive only as conversational vocabulary, never as products.** With Guided parked, the live external dial spans two notches ("with your team" → "we run it all"). We still walk into the call with *one* number for them — the grid supplies discrete internal anchors; the customer never shops a slider.

## Continuation — upmarket + the consult front-door (2026-06-24)

*Exploration, not decision. Picks up the thread above. The 1–10 founder-led motion from steps 12–16 stands unchanged and proven; this section opens a second, deliberately experimental track aimed higher.*

17. **Revenue range pushed up → 10–50M ARR floated, with a correction baked in.** Reasoning: an established 10–50M company has durable reasons to invest in organic (long-term channel, already convinced of the value). The correction: established also means they *already have* SEO — an agency or in-house team — so for the **SEO** work we're a *rip-and-replace of an incumbent*, which is slower, more cautious, and more reference-hungry than the founder-led reallocation. Landed: the range extends upward, but the upmarket move is **gated, not immediate** (see step 21), and split into two internal bands — **1–10M** and **10–50M** — as *segment sizing*, NOT a return to two external SKUs (step 16 still holds).

18. **AEO is the leveler — the unlock for the testimonial problem.** Key realization: *nobody has AEO case studies yet.* The whole market is at zero, so the logo-wall expectation that makes upmarket SEO a knife fight largely **evaporates in AEO** — there's no incumbent and no established playbook to be measured against. You compete on thesis and on being visibly ahead, not on "who have you done this for." This corrects the earlier "wait 6–12 months for proof" worry: that applies to the **SEO rip-and-replace**, not to AEO. **Lead with AEO (open land grab, no references needed); expand into SEO/organic once inside and trusted.**

19. **The strength, put in buyer language.** Not the fuzzy "we're AI-native." The concrete wedge (already in the price grid's intelligence layer): we can see *which sources the LLMs actually pull the category's answers from*, run experiments against it, and act at a speed/cost no human agency or in-house team can match — because they're SEO-era shops bolting AI on, and in AEO their decade of SEO experience is partly a *liability*, not a moat. The sellable sentence is **"your agency literally cannot see what we see,"** not "nobody's happy with their agency" (true, but every challenger says it).

20. **The two new ideas are one motion — the AEO-led consult is the testimonial-free front-door to 10–50.** High-ticket consulting sells on the *founder's expertise and thesis*, not on case studies — so it gets us in the door of a bigger company without the references we lack. Inside, we install **the product as the substrate** (not pure advisory, not their stack), build trust, and the managed/agency work we can't sell cold becomes a *warm expansion* — the same land-and-expand we already believe in, with a consult as the front-door instead of a cold $2k offer. This also revives the **parked Guided tier**: Guided couldn't be sold because the product can't yet self-run unattended — but it's not a walk-away if *the founder is in the room stabilizing it*. Founder-led install = Guided-shaped work sellable **today**. **Three guardrails so this doesn't rot into an agency:** (a) **substrate = our product**, somewhere we control — be willing to lose the deal that demands "install into our stack"; (b) **productize + price to convert** — fixed scope, fixed deliverable, designed to *end on the managed dial*, not renew as more advisory; (c) **every install must feed reusable backlog** (the execution ledger) or it's just a job the founder bought himself.

21. **Trigger, not calendar.** Killed "do 1–10 for a few weeks, then jump." SEO/AEO results take *months* to surface, and the upmarket SEO sale needs *referenceable results*, not just closed logos. So the SEO-side upmarket expansion is gated on a **trigger** ("2 referenceable accounts showing [specific result]"), never a date. AEO-side entry is **now** (no proof exists for anyone). Document the destination; don't lie about the timeline.

22. **Two sharp bets, deliberately not 50/50 — the engine is observed, not pre-chosen.** Per step 11's own discipline (sharp bets, pre-committed signal + kill date, let the market reveal the engine). **Bet A — 1–10M, cold Co-Pilot offer = baseline cash / the dog *for now*:** proven, low-effort, keep it fed. **Bet B — 10–50M, AEO-led consult front-door = the experiment** where net-new energy goes; give it a signal + kill date (illustrative: "3 discovery dinners → 1 paid consult in 8 weeks, else drop"). **Build nothing infrastructural** — no new website or brand for an unsold motion; **reach out as an individual** (the founder's name + AI track record *is* the credential). **Consult aimed at 10–50 only;** keep the 1–10 cold offer clean so the two signals stay readable. We don't crown an engine — we run the proven thing on autopilot and let close-rate, margin, and the founder's own energy decide in ~2 months on data, not vibes today.

## Continuation — the bootstrapped-solo reframe & the execution plan (2026-06-24, later same day)

*Picks up the thread above. The constraints got made explicit for the first time, and they broke part of the steps 17–22 plan. Same format: where we were, the turn, where we landed.*

23. **Constraints stated → they reorder everything.** For the first time the operating constraints were named explicitly: **bootstrapped, profitability as fast as possible, lean (solo founder + maybe an intern), and a closing window** (AEO anxiety is peaking now with no playbook; in ~12–18 months agencies bolt on an AEO checkbox and the greenfield closes; the founder's AI-native speed edge also commoditizes). These don't just add color — they invalidate parts of the steps 17–22 plan, which implicitly assumed more hands and more time. Everything below is the correction.

24. **Consult demoted: experiment → deal-shape toolkit.** The step 22 plan put net-new energy into the AEO consult (Bet B). Correction: the consult is *anti-leverage for a solo founder*. "Sells on the founder's expertise" means only the founder can sell *or* deliver it, so it consumes the one resource that can't be spared — build time — exactly when the product must mature; and it's bespoke, so it doesn't compound. Landed: **the consult is not an experiment. It stays in the toolkit as a deal-shape we deploy when a specific deal's economics justify it** (big ACV, a 10–50M logo worth having, a high-touch-only buyer) — and when deployed it's **tagged off-experiment** so it doesn't contaminate the signal.

25. **Two sharp bets collapse to one engine.** Step 22 ran Bet A (1–10M) and Bet B (10–50M) in parallel. Solo, that's infeasible: ~10–15 hrs/week of real selling capacity can't feed two bets, so each gets half-fed, signal is confounded, and you kill the wrong one — the exact failure step 11 warned against. Landed: **one primary hunt + inbound-only for everything else.**

26. **The missing rung: a productized AEO audit as the front-door.** The plan had a cold $2k offer and a high-touch consult, with nothing productized between them. Added: an **AEO Visibility Report** ("where does your brand show up when buyers ask ChatGPT / Claude / Perplexity / Google AI about your category, who's cited instead of you, and the 90-day fix"). It clears every constraint at once — sells cold *without testimonials* (it's a diagnosis, not a promise), it **is** the wedge demonstration ("your agency can't even see this" becomes a deliverable they hold), it rides the closing window precisely, it qualifies-and-pays-you-to-qualify, it's **productizable so it escapes the solo bottleneck**, and it feeds one funnel for both revenue bands. The audit becomes top-of-funnel: cold outreach sells the audit; the audit sells the managed dial.

27. **Audit pricing decoupled from cost → a ladder, not a number.** The automated audit costs ~$20–30 to produce; that's **irrelevant** to price. Price is set by the audit's *job in the funnel*, and it can do two jobs via a ladder: **(a) free automated teaser** — given away in cold outreach, protects top-of-funnel *and* is the wedge demo; **(b) paid deep audit ~$500–1,500** — the qualifier + commitment device + first cash, and the place an FDM/intern adds human depth; **(c) managed retainer $2k (Co-Pilot) / $4k (Fully-Run)**. Hard rule: **sequence human labor behind intent** — never pour comprehensive FDM hours onto unqualified cold leads, or you've rebuilt the Fully-Run margin trap at the top of the funnel. The earlier confusion ("will a $2k audit fly?") was a category error: $2k is the *retainer*, never the cold audit.

28. **The attribution reframe — the cube collapses to one live axis.** The experiment was drifting toward three axes varied at once (vertical × revenue band × outreach angle) = a confounded cube. The unlock: **two of the three are already decided, so they're constants, not variables.** *Angle* is decided (audit is the default front-door; consult is the per-deal exception) → not varied. *Band* is decided (hunt 1–10M; 10–50M is opportunistic/inbound) → not varied. **Vertical is the only live experiment variable.** So "few weeks then switch" is legitimate *only when the switch is along the vertical axis*; switching multiple axes is the randomness we're trying to escape. Mechanics (Close CRM via the dogfu pipeline): stamp every lead with `vertical`, `band`, `angle`, `experiment_vs_opportunistic`. The read: *among experiment leads only (band=1–10M, angle=audit), which vertical has the best audit-sell rate and audit→managed conversion?* All opportunistic revenue (10–50M inbound, consult deals, no-team SMB) is **banked as cash but excluded from the vertical read.** This separation is the whole game — it lets us take any revenue that walks in without polluting the one signal we're paying to read.

29. **Fully-Run reframe — not a trap to avoid; a strategic R&D + proof engine (founder's correction).** The advisor's earlier "avoid Fully-Run" was too flat. The correction (founder's): even though FR doesn't expose the product to the end user, **the FDMs executing the work is the fastest product-feedback loop** (gaps surface in real use; the execution ledger fills with exactly the backlog the product is built to absorb), it's the **case-study factory** (controlled outcome → a referenceable win), and it carries **higher ACV**. That's three of the four things we most lack pre-PMF, all from one motion. So the verdict isn't "avoid FR" — it's **never run *subsidized* FR for the wrong customer** (the $299 no-team clinic was the trap: full labor, no margin, no strategic payoff). At full freight ($4k floor) for a paying scaleup that wants hands-off, FR is customer-funded product development. Landed: **hunt Co-Pilot for cash; deliberately keep 1–2 full-price Fully-Run accounts as the R&D-and-proof engine** — they generate the Stage-2 PMF signal fastest. Tension held consciously, not resolved: **Co-Pilot = margin per unit of our effort today; Fully-Run = product progress + proof + ACV.** Don't let FR accounts outnumber Co-Pilot (labor-heavy, one person), but a couple may buy down more PMF risk than five Co-Pilot logos.

30. **PMF defined, and a staged path with mechanical gates.** We kept aiming at "PMF" without defining it. For a product-funded-by-service hybrid, **PMF = three things true at once: (a) a repeatable cold→audit→managed motion that closes at the floor without reinventing the pitch; (b) delivery contribution ≥60%; (c) the product absorbing the execution layer — founder-hours-per-account falling as accounts are added (the cannibalization line moving).** Without (c) it's a profitable services business, not a product company — a legitimate choice, but name it. Stages: **S1 Repeatable LAND** (one vertical, audit→Co-Pilot; illustrative gate: sell/deliver ~8–12 audits → convert ≥3 to managed at the $2k floor within 8 weeks; banks vertical source-map v1); **S2 Repeatable EXPAND + margin holds** (≥60% contribution, ≥1 land-and-expand event, founder-hours/account trending down); **S3 PMF** (the motion runs without the founder being the load-bearing element → only then decide: scale bootstrapped or raise). The bet/kill-date discipline from step 11 is preserved, just compressed to one engine with numeric gates so "working/not working" stays mechanical.

## Continuation — differentiation in a saturated landscape (2026-06-24, retro)

*A founder retro surfaced the sharpest unsolved problem: the landscape is brutally competitive — agencies and SEO shops everywhere, and **almost all of them already offer a free or paid audit.** If our front-door (§3 of the execution plan) is "another audit," we're invisible. Candidate ideas thrown on the table: the consult; a free Claude/Codex skills package; our open-source crawler (better than ScreamingFrog); "make your team AI-native" webinars. The question asked was for the *lenses to decide by*, not the specifics.*

31. **The reframe: you don't out-shout a red ocean, you out-narrow it.** A bigger free thing makes you *legible as "one of those,"* not different. Differentiation in a crowded market comes from being **narrower, more specific, and on-wedge** — the test for any idea is "what can *I* credibly do or say that the 1,000 agencies structurally cannot." Separated three jobs that the candidate ideas were conflating: **Message** (what you say in the touch — mostly missing from the idea list; the real message differentiator is the **wedge finding delivered before they ask**: "I asked ChatGPT/Perplexity to recommend a tool in your category — it named these competitors, not you, and here's the source it trusted." Intelligence, not an offer; agencies can't produce it); **Proof** (why they believe — the **open-source crawler** + **skills package** = credibility infra signaling *builder, not marketer*; the reason the hook is believable, not the hook); **Motion** (how you reach — cold, webinars, build-in-public, OSS distribution).

32. **Four lenses + two bigger levers → the conclusion.** Lenses, in priority order for our constraints: (1) **compounds vs evaporates** — favor assets (crawler, skills, reusable per-vertical finding engine) over **time-for-attention trades** (webinar, consult) that burn the scarce founder and leave nothing behind; (2) **demonstrates the wedge vs decorates** (a generic "AI-native" webinar is off-thesis); (3) **attracts ICP-1 specifically vs a generic crowd** (OSS + build-in-public is catnip for technical founders; broad webinars pull low-intent crowds + competitors); (4) **copyable by an agency or not** — our most defensible, authentic edge is that **we are a builder and they are marketers cosplaying AI-native**; lead from the one axis they can't cross. Two bigger levers surfaced: **niche depth IS differentiation** (same problem as §6 verticalization — "the AEO person for *[one vertical]*" beats "an AI SEO agency," and a hyper-specific finding beats a generic audit), and **cold vs distribution-led motion** (the crawler/skills/build-in-public are really *distribution* plays that make later outreach *warm* — decide which is the **engine** for near-term cash and which is the **flywheel** compounding underneath). **Landed: compete on the *finding* + the *identity (builder, not marketer)* + the *niche* — not on the audit.** Crawler & skills = proof/distribution to invest in; webinar & consult = the two to be most skeptical of (burn the founder, don't compound).

## Continuation — constraints made real, the customer correction, and the 8-week sprint (2026-06-25)

*The biggest turn yet: the constraints got *quantified* and the founder's actual situation got stated, and it surfaced that everything in steps 1–32 was reasoned with **zero market contact.** Same format: where we were, the turn, where we landed.*

33. **The whole framework was theory — admitted.** For the first time it was named: the founder had been **head-down building** and did **no sales/marketing until 4 weeks ago.** The cold outreach tried since then was **un-personalized and the response was very bad.** So steps 1–32 are an elegant theory of a market not yet talked to. Not wrong — just now outranked by even the small real evidence below. **Runway = 8 weeks**, extendable ~**10 more months via a loan** *if* there are good signals. So the S1 gate and the death clock are **the same 8-week clock** — there is no slack the staged plan implied.

34. **The customer-growth claim was false → corrected.** Earlier docs leaned on "our customer went 20k→100k MRR **while using us**" / "our **5x customer is the proof**." Untrue: that growth happened **last year, before us**; they became a client only **2 weeks ago**, already at that revenue. **That customer is NOT a results case study.** What it *is*: a **referral source** and **one true pitch-credibility signal** — they evaluated a **$4k agency they liked and chose us instead.** Rule banked: **keep only the proof that survives contact;** delete the rest.

35. **Delivery isn't the bottleneck — founder time is, and it has a threshold.** Partnered with an agency for **elastic FDM capacity ($20/hr, scalable to 20–30 FT)**. Unit-economics worry **deliberately deferred** (agency is a pre-revenue stopgap; move in-house as project count grows). **Product is ready for the first 3 customers — no more building needed.** Founder time splits **~80% sellable / ~20% mediating the agency FDMs**, and the mediation load is relieved structurally **at 3 customers** (the point a dedicated client-handling FDM can be hired).

36. **The magic number is 3.** One target satisfies **four unlocks at once**: (a) runway **cash**, (b) the **signal that justifies the loan**, (c) the **dedicated-FDM hire** that relieves the time bottleneck, (d) **n=3 replication** of the ICP. Everything in the 8 weeks subordinates to **"land 3 ICP-fit customers at the $2k floor."**

37. **The motion inverts: warm-primary, not cold-audit-primary.** The only real evidence — **one warm-referral close; cold bombed** — outranks the theory, and the theory had made the *cold audit* the engine. So for the sprint, **warm founder-to-founder referral is the primary channel.** Funnel math confirms a second channel is needed, not optional: **~10–15 warm intros × 20–30% ≈ 2–4 closes** = the target of 3 with **zero margin for error.** Second channel = **insurance**, not greed.

38. **Two channels, not three — and the right second one.** Founder wanted a second channel (correct) and floated **audit-led cold** *or* **education/consulting**. **Education parked** — by our own §8 logic it burns the scarce founder, compounds slowly, and pulls low-intent crowds (a flywheel, not an 8-week engine; and a back-door for the build instinct). Chosen second channel: **personalized wedge-finding cold** — *"I asked ChatGPT/Perplexity to recommend a [category] tool; it named [competitor A] and [B], not you; here's the source it trusted."* **The finding IS the personalization,** which fixes exactly why the first cold attempt failed; it tests the core message directly and feeds the per-vertical source map. **The audit is recast:** a **sales artifact inside conversations** + the free wedge demo — **not** the engine, **not** a paid revenue rung during the sprint.

39. **Channel/vertical reconciliation + loan discipline.** The §2 "vertical is the only live variable" discipline bends honestly: the **warm channel can't control vertical** (you take the ICP-fit founders your network gives you → vertical **opportunistic**), so the clean **vertical experiment runs on the wedge-cold channel**, which *can* be pointed. **Qualify the 10–15 warm intros against the ICP first** — a warm intro to a no-team / no-SEO-spend founder is just a cold lead wearing a friend's name. And **pre-commit the loan go/no-go now**, before optimism colors it (illustrative: *"3 closed at floor + a reproducing referral chain = take the loan; less than that = do not add debt, reassess"*).

## The decided model, in one paragraph

One business, one funnel. Externally: a managed AI-marketing offer with a setup front-door and a self-serve ceiling, the full capability surface shown to everyone, and the FDM as the single elastic customer-facing role. Pricing: a platform base + coverage sold as scope (costed in hours internally, never billed as hours), across three coverage tiers (Guided / Co-Pilot / Fully-Run) and three scale bands (small / mid / large surface) — a 9-cell internal grid the customer never sees. The customer makes one decision (coverage) and goes through one scoping step (scale); the price is reached on a call. Underneath it all runs the execution ledger and the cannibalization line that keep this a product company funded by service, not a services company with better tooling.
