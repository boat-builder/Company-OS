# Content & Outbound Playbook

> Berlin's content philosophy, audience discipline, funnel framing, execution craft, and outbound-from-content motion. Operational guide — **not canonical**. Canonical facts about which channels Berlin uses and what each one is for live in `../canonical/marketing.md`. ICP definitions live in `../canonical/customers.md`. Sales-call positioning and objection handling live in `../canonical/sales.md`.

---

## Preconditions

This playbook assumes the following are already true. If any of them aren't, fix that before applying the rest of this file — the playbook will not compensate for a missing precondition, it will only accelerate whatever is underneath.

- **Product-market fit is real.** Content amplifies existing demand; it does not create it. Distribution accelerates what is already true — including weak PMF. If buyers don't already want what Berlin sells, no amount of posting will fix that.
- **The founder is committed to posting.** Content has to live where trust exists — founder and executive accounts, not the company page. If founder posting cadence isn't real, this playbook stalls.
- **Channel and audience decisions exist upstream.** Which channels Berlin uses lives in `../canonical/marketing.md`. ICP definitions live in `../canonical/customers.md`. This playbook assumes those decisions are made (or that the "consistency over precision" fallback in Audience Definition applies while ICP is still being sharpened).

## Core Principles

- **Core messaging and positioning must be intact** throughout the content. The product narrative does not bend per channel or per post.
- **Content is the GTM infrastructure.** It is not adjacent to GTM; it is GTM.
- **Convert sales call transcripts and ideas into narratives** that fit the positioning and backstory. Treat raw conversation material as content fuel.
- **Content is not where you sell. It is where you earn the right to sell later.** The CTA is the conversation that follows, not the post itself.

## Audience Definition

Before publishing, answer clearly: **who is this for?**

- A defined role or buying persona.
- A defined company profile.
- A defined set of problems they recognize as urgent.

Know who you are speaking to at the narrowest level possible. It is fine to have multiple ICPs. Decide which industry, segment, or buyer you want to dominate and make that obvious in every post.

The current ICP set lives in `../canonical/customers.md`. Reference it before drafting; do not improvise audience.

If commitment to a vertical stalls, **prioritize consistency over precision**. It is better to post regularly to a slightly broader audience than to wait for perfect ICP clarity. Buy-in and momentum matter more than theoretical optimization.

## Content Goals

Every piece of content should have a primary goal. **Do not mix objectives within the same post.**

- **Brand Positioning.** Earn attention through strong opinions, sharp market commentary, and clear points of view. Make the right audience stop scrolling and recognize how you think.
- **Pipeline Generation.** Reduce uncertainty. Demonstrate execution. Make your approach legible. Provide buyers with confidence. Use tactical breakdowns, system explanations, case studies, and milestone posts.

---

## Content Funnel

The funnel is not about calls to action. **It is about specificity.**

- **TOFU.** Speak broadly to a role or industry. Identify shared pain, market shifts, or emerging patterns. Attract the right audience and filter out the wrong one.
- **MOFU.** Speak directly to your ICP's lived experience. Demonstrate operational-level understanding. This is where trust builds quietly.
- **BOFU.** Be highly specific. Reduce risk. Show momentum. Make your execution visible. Examples: case studies, hiring announcements, product updates tied to customer demand, system explanations, revenue milestones framed as validation.

**Balance the funnel deliberately.** Most companies overproduce TOFU because likes and comments feel like progress. But pipeline is built disproportionately in MOFU and BOFU. A healthy content system spans all three levels intentionally.

---

## Content Requirements

Every post should do at least one of three things:

- **Educate.**
- **Relate.**
- **Signal momentum.**

If it does none of those, do not publish it.

---

## Execution

Strong content gets three elements right:

- The hook earns attention.
- The body delivers value.
- The structure reinforces clarity.

Most effort should go into the hook and the clarity of the argument. **If the hook fails, the body will never be read.** Once attention is earned, the body's job is to justify that attention.

## Outbound from Content

Every interaction with content is an opportunity for outbound because the lead is warm.

**What "warm" means.** Warm does not mean someone asked for a demo. It means the prospect already has context — they engaged with a post, viewed your profile, connected after observing your content, commented or liked, or have visibly consumed your thinking over time. Treating these signals as cold leads wastes the leverage that content created.

**Timing matters more than scripting.** The window opens the moment someone engages. Best outreach goes out within hours, not weeks — context decays fast.

When you outbound:

- Reference the specific trigger for outreach.
- Provide something useful or relevant.
- Avoid leading with a meeting request.

Lead with insight. Offer a perspective that builds on what they engaged with. Provide value that would be worthwhile even if no call is booked.

For every piece of content, have a **"conversation carrier"** built and ready that eventually opens up the sales conversation. **Personalization is not optional — automation destroys warm outbound.** Sending a templated message to someone who just engaged with thoughtful content erodes trust immediately. Every message should feel intentional.
