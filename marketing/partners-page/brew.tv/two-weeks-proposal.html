A two-week diagnostic for brew.tv

A focused look at why ~200K of your pages are stuck in “crawled, currently not indexed,” and what we’d do about it.

Below is what we found in a short look at brew.tv after our call, the hypothesis we’d test, and the shape of the engagement. The aim is to give you something concrete you can say yes or no to.

What we saw in a quick look

We spent about half an hour on brew.tv after our call — enough to look at a few page types, but not enough to see what’s actually happening inside GSC. The technical fundamentals look OK on the surface: pages return 200, render server-side, carry self-referential canonicals, and titles on streamable films read reasonably (Ghich Pich (2025) – Watch Exclusively on Brew). But one pattern stood out — the kind a generic audit tool wouldn’t flag.

Pages don’t clearly signal “watch” intent, even when you stream the film

Body content on both streamable and non-streamable films follows a similar template: plot synopsis + critics’ reviews scraped from third parties + structured data. The differentiation between “a page where you watch this film” and “a page about this film” is subtle.

A rough count of streaming-intent terms (rent / buy / subscribe / watch now / streaming / $) on two pages of similar size:

  • Ghich Pich (streamed in India): 9 occurrences
  • Ema (not streamed in India): 3 occurrences

Brew is differentiating — but 9 vs 3 on a 280KB page is a soft signal. For Googlebot trying to tell whether a URL is a destination or an info page, the line is fuzzy. Under Google’s post-2024 Helpful Content and scaled-content-abuse systems, low-info-gain pages at scale create a site-wide quality drag that can keep them stuck in “crawled, currently not indexed” indefinitely.

Working hypothesis

The clearest pattern from a surface inspection is information-gain at scale — too many pages reading as templated wrappers around third-party data, without enough unique value or unmistakable user-intent signal. That fits the symptom (large blocks of pages crawled but not indexed) and matches a well-documented post-2024 Google behaviour.

But this is one hypothesis from thirty minutes of looking. The things we can’t see from outside — and that often matter as much or more — include canonical and sitemap composition across /in/, /us/, and other locales; which URL patterns sit in “crawled, currently not indexed” vs “discovered, currently not indexed”; rendering and crawl-budget behaviour over time in GSC; and how the indexed-vs-discovered ratio is trending week-on-week.

The point of the diagnostic is to look at those alongside the info-gain hypothesis and tell you which actually drives your indexation gap — instead of guessing from the outside.

The two weeks

Investigation and synthesis run together rather than splitting into two distinct phases. Four work streams across the engagement:

A
Bucket the 200K. Pull the unindexed URLs from GSC and group them by likely cause — redirect waste, render failure, low info gain, canonical/sitemap quirks, technical errors. The shape of the split often points to the real driver.
B
Berlin audits at scale. Alongside the bucketing work, Berlin runs a site-wide pass to surface the long tail of secondary GSC issues — 404s, redirect chains, canonical conflicts, sitemap drift.
C
Find the content wins. A focused look at the small set of pages where adding genuine information gain — regional availability, cast/crew context, watch FAQs, age guidance — could move indexation and ranking together. Where the win is obvious, we work through the specifics rather than just listing the candidate.
D
Synthesise + walk through. Write up what we found and walk through it together at the end of week two.

No fixes are shipped during these two weeks — the deliverable is a clear, evidence-backed picture you can act on, internally or with us.
