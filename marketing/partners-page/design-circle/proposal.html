02

Search is a different job now

The thing most people still picture as “SEO” — put the right keywords on the page so Google ranks you first — is now a small slice of the work. Two things changed, and they're why baking search into the redesign now is the smart move.

The surface area exploded

It used to be one engine: Google. Now buyers and clinicians get answers from Google's traditional results, Google AI Mode, AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and more — each sourcing from different places. Google is still dominant, but it has fallen from ~85%+ to roughly 60–70% as AI assistants take share.

The work moved off the page

A few years ago ~70% of the effort lived on your own website. Today roughly 60–65% lives off it — because AI answers are assembled from sources the model already trusts, which usually means other people's pages, not yours.

The extra work that now has to happen

  • Optimizing for multiple AI engines, each sourcing answers differently.
  • Prompt research — the actual questions buyers ask AI, at scale (thousands of variations), not just keywords.
  • Citation-source reverse-engineering — for each answer, finding where the AI pulls from, then working those exact sources.
  • Community operations — e.g. a genuine Reddit strategy (aged accounts, karma, no API, heavy moderation) when Reddit dominates a topic.
  • AI-crawler hygiene — making sure the bots behind ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI can actually read the site.
  • Schema / structured data per page type, so engines understand the brand as an entity.
  • Continuous monitoring & re-work — the source mix changes weekly; a one-time project goes stale fast.
  • Controlled experimentation — testing approaches across cohorts of pages without tanking live traffic.

That 7–8× increase in surface area is a real increase in work. A traditional agency either multiplies its team and its price to cover it — or, far more often, doesn't cover it at all and quietly stays inside the old on-page checklist.

03

On-page vs. off-page, explained plainly

So you can present it without miscommunicating. Two layers — and the order matters.

Layer 1 · Non-negotiable

On-page — your website

  • Technical health — readable by search engines and AI crawlers, fast, doesn't break, renders without depending on JavaScript, isn't blocking AI bots.
  • Content & structure — the right pages, targeting the right terms and questions, with the schema that makes them citable.
  • Positioning consistency — one tight, identical description of the brand, everywhere it appears.

This is the foundation. If it's weak, off-page delivers nothing — and it's exactly what we lock in during the website build, before launch.

Layer 2 · Where the modern game is won

Off-page — everywhere else

What other credible sources say about the brand — now the majority of the work, and brand-specific, not a checklist.

  • Backlinks — relevant sites linking to you, earned through outreach (and sometimes paid placement).
  • The sources AI actually cites — Reddit for one topic, a niche blog for another, YouTube or LinkedIn for a third. We find where the AI sources its answer, then go work those places.

Real example: for a hormone-therapeutics brand we work with, Reddit was only ~0.3% of relevant results — so we skipped it and went straight to the two or three specialist blogs that dominated, working with their owners directly.

How this fits your website build

Your runway is roughly two months to a live site — but most of the work doesn't have to wait for it. A surprising amount starts now, in parallel with the redesign.

Starts now · before launch

Foundation + off-page, in parallel

  • Hand your web team a concrete technical spec — rendering, crawlability, structured data, AI-bot access, site structure — and review content for search-friendliness before it's written in.
  • Build the positioning doc (see the next section) so the brand reads consistently everywhere.
  • Begin off-page community building — Reddit or LinkedIn, depending on where this audience lives. Earning presence in the sources AI cites doesn't need the website to exist yet, and it's the slowest-compounding work — so starting early matters most.
At / after launch

On the live domain

On-site execution lands on the new site, and continuous optimization — citations, monitoring, and the experiment loop below — runs at full speed.

How we run it — the three-phase loop

Not a one-time project; a continuous loop you watch in the dashboard.

Phase 1

Positioning (foundation)

Berlin builds a brand profile from the web; the marketer corrects what the agents get wrong; you approve. The output is a positioning doc that defines how the brand should read across the internet — not just your own site — so neither people nor AI models get a confused or contradictory picture. This is the single, consistent story everything else compounds from.

Phase 2

Audit

Two streams in parallel — technical/on-page health, and competitive analysis — plus a pass that maps what people actually search and the AI prompts they ask, turned into a prioritized target list we bring back for your approval.

Phase 3

Report → Act

Findings become a living report and always-on workflows — a typical brand runs ~50–150 monitoring jobs watching the site, competitors and AI presence. From those, the marketer generates and executes the work; anything touching the live site is surfaced for review first.

Who owns what: you approve every direction; Berlin owns the execution and the expertise — with every run, finding, and action visible in the dashboard.
