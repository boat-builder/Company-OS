## 1. Audience Snapshot

> _Brief summary for marketing context. For full ICP detail, see [ICP 1 Profile](/customers/profiles/icp-1-agency-medium.md) and [ICP 2 Profile](/customers/profiles/icp-2-agency-large.md)._

SEO and marketing agencies managing 5 to 100+ client accounts. They sell SEO services — audits, reporting, content strategy, rank tracking, competitive analysis — and their profitability depends on delivering these at scale without proportional headcount growth. They're operationally sophisticated but technically constrained: they know exactly what they'd automate if they could, but they don't have developers. The ones who've tried DIY automation (n8n, Make, Claude API) hit walls on API authentication, hallucination, and maintenance. They're under competitive pressure to offer AI search visibility (GEO/AEO) to clients but most are winging it or avoiding the conversation.

**Key messaging implications from ICP profiles:**

- The buyer is usually the Agency Owner or Director of SEO — they think in terms of margin, capacity, and client retention, not features.
- They compare Berlin to a headcount ($60K–$120K/year per hire), not a software subscription. Frame accordingly.
- Pre-built workflows are a stronger initial hook than the custom builder — they don't have time to build from scratch.
- Client-facing outputs matter as much as internal efficiency. Reports, audits, and dashboards need to look professional and carry the agency's brand.

## 3. The Problem Narrative

_Use this framing in decks, landing page hero sections, and top-of-funnel content._

You started an agency to do great SEO work. Now you spend most of your time on reporting, rank tracking, crawl analysis, and the same manual processes repeated for every single client. Your team is drowning in deliverables, not doing strategy.

Every new client means more of the same work. You can't grow without hiring, and hiring eats your margin. You're paying for Ahrefs, Semrush, Screaming Frog, a rank tracker, a reporting tool — five or six subscriptions that don't talk to each other. Your data lives in a dozen places. Every report is a bespoke manual job.

Meanwhile, SEO is changing faster than you can keep up. Clients are asking about AI search — "Are we showing up in ChatGPT? What about Perplexity?" — and you don't have a good answer. Competitors are pitching "AI SEO capabilities" and you're falling behind.

You've thought about automation. Maybe you've tried n8n or Make. But every path turns into an engineering project — API authentication, node chaining, debugging broken workflows. And when you do get something running, the AI hallucinates on complex data and the output isn't trustworthy enough to send to a client.

The result: your best people spend their days on work that should be automated, you're leaving money on the table by not offering AI search services, and you can't grow without burning out your team or destroying your margin.

You don't need another tool. You need infrastructure that lets your agency operate at twice the capacity with the same team.

## 4. Messaging Pillars

_Ordered by priority — lead with Pillar 1 in all primary messaging. Pillars 1–3 are the core story for agencies. Pillars 4–6 are strong secondary hooks._

### Pillar 1 [PRIMARY]: "Automate the 70% — free your team for the work that actually matters"

This is the umbrella message. Agencies report that roughly 70% of their client work is manual, repeatable, and mundane — rank tracking, crawl analysis, keyword research, reporting, competitive monitoring. This is the work that eats margin and prevents growth. Berlin automates it.

Build an agent in a few minutes through a simple conversation. That agent automates work that used to consume hours — for one client or across all of them. The math is straightforward: if your team spends 15–20 hours per week per client on manual SEO work, and 70% of that is automatable, you've just freed up 10–14 hours per client per week. That's capacity for more clients, or more strategic work for the clients you have.

**The proof point:** One agency used Berlin to build 3 agents in 2 hours for an ecommerce client with 2.5 million URLs. Those agents — a unified multi-source weekly report, rank tracking across 100 keywords in 6 countries, and a smart 404 redirection engine that matched 12,000 URLs to the most appropriate destination — replaced an estimated 120 hours of manual work. Two hours of setup. 120 hours saved. That's the ratio.

**Key features to reference:** Agentic Workflow Builder, Agentic Workflow Marketplace, Workflow Scheduling, Workflow Sharing

**Sub-messages within this pillar:**

- **Build once, run across all clients.** An agent built for one client's reporting works across every client account. The marginal cost of adding a new client drops to near zero.
- **Pre-built workflows get you value on day one.** The workflow marketplace has vetted, ready-to-run agents for the most common agency tasks — no building required. Customize later if you want, or just run them.
- **Speed is the differentiator.** An agent that takes 5 minutes to build automates hours of weekly work. Every day you don't have it running, your team is doing that work manually.

### Pillar 2 [PRIMARY]: "Automated proposals and SEO audits — turn enquiries into clients faster"

Agencies get a steady stream of inbound enquiries, especially as SEO is changing with AI search. For every enquiry, they need to produce a quote backed by an SEO audit — site crawl, keyword analysis, competitor landscape, technical issues. This process takes hours per prospect and it's the bottleneck between an enquiry and a closed deal.

Berlin can automate the entire proposal workflow with a single agent: prospect submits their URL, the agent crawls their site, pulls keyword data, identifies technical issues, benchmarks against competitors, and generates a branded audit report. What used to take half a day takes minutes. More enquiries get processed, faster. More prospects become clients.

**Key features to reference:** Agentic Workflow Builder, Site Crawling & Competitor Monitoring, Keyword Intelligence, Brand Context, Report Center

### Pillar 3 [PRIMARY]: "Schedule agents, send reports — the work runs while you sleep"

Agencies need recurring deliverables — weekly reports, monthly audits, daily rank checks, competitor alerts. Berlin's scheduled agents run automatically at whatever cadence you set and deliver results to the Report Center. Your team reviews and sends to clients. The manual ritual of "pull data, build report, format, send" becomes "review and send."

Combine this with Berlin's brand context feature — which stores each client's brand guidelines, terminology, and preferences — and the reports come out client-ready, carrying the agency's branding, without anyone formatting a slide deck at midnight.

**Key features to reference:** Workflow Scheduling, Report Center, Brand Context, Review Center

### Pillar 4 [SECONDARY]: "One platform replaces your entire toolstack"

Agencies are typically paying for 4–6 separate subscriptions — keyword tool, crawl tool, rank tracker, reporting tool, maybe a separate GEO monitoring service. At agency scale, this adds up to $500–$2,000+/month. None of it connects. Berlin's integrated keyword intelligence, proprietary crawlers, and expanding integrations replace the stack. One subscription. All data in one place. All agents built on top of the same unified layer.

**Key features to reference:** Keyword Intelligence, Site Crawling & Competitor Monitoring, Unified Data Access Layer, Third-Party Integrations

### Pillar 5 [SECONDARY]: "Offer AI search visibility — a new service your competitors can't match"

Clients are asking about AI search and most agencies don't have answers. Berlin's GEO/AEO capabilities let agencies monitor and optimize client visibility across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and other AI surfaces. This isn't just a feature — it's a new revenue line. Package it as a premium service, charge for it separately, and differentiate from every competitor who's still only talking about traditional rankings.

**Key features to reference:** Ranking Signal Intelligence, Agentic Workflow Marketplace (GEO/AEO workflows)

### Pillar 6 [SECONDARY]: "Manage all clients, all team members, one workspace"

Every client project, every team member, every agent — managed from a single workspace. Brand context per client keeps outputs consistent regardless of who on the team runs the workflow. Team management with access controls means the right people see the right projects. No more scattered logins, siloed data, or inconsistent deliverables.

**Key features to reference:** Team & Org Management, Brand Context, Workflow Sharing, Review Center

## 5. What Resonates — Field-Validated Messaging Signals

> _For the raw discovery intel behind these conclusions, see the Discovery Intel tables in [ICP 1 Profile](/customers/profiles/icp-1-agency-medium.md) and [ICP 2 Profile](/customers/profiles/icp-2-agency-large.md)._

### Features that get the strongest reaction

1. **Automated proposal/audit generation** — the ability to turn an inbound enquiry into a branded SEO audit with a single agent is the immediate "I need this" moment. Agencies spend hours on every proposal; this collapses that to minutes.
2. **Scheduled agents with branded reports** — recurring deliverables that run themselves and come out looking like the agency's own work. This is the operational relief they've been looking for.
3. **The 70% number** — agencies recognize immediately that the vast majority of their client work is manual and repeatable. "70% of what you do is automatable" is a powerful framing that resonates because they live it every day.
4. **The 2-hour / 120-hour proof point** — 3 agents built in 2 hours saving 120 hours of work. This is concrete, specific, and hard to argue with. Use it prominently.
5. **Unlimited projects at one price** — agencies think in terms of per-client economics. Knowing the platform scales across all their clients without per-project charges removes a major friction point.
6. **Build once, run across all clients** — the realization that a workflow built for one client works for all of them changes the ROI math entirely.

### How this maps to pillar priority

The pillar ordering reflects field signals. Pillar 1 (automate the 70%) is the umbrella that addresses the core pain — agency teams drowning in manual work that prevents growth. Pillar 2 (automated proposals) addresses the specific bottleneck that agencies feel most acutely with every new enquiry. Pillar 3 (scheduled agents and reports) speaks to the operational dream of deliverables that produce themselves. Secondary pillars (toolstack consolidation, AI search services, workspace management) are real value but not the initial hook.

### Selling motion note

This audience is pragmatic and ROI-oriented. They don't need category education about automation or AI — they need to see that it works, that it's fast, and that it pays for itself. Lead with the proof point (2 hours → 120 hours saved), show a live demo of an agent being built, and let them calculate the math on their own client roster. The sale happens when they multiply the per-client time savings by their number of clients.

## 8. Pricing Framing

_How to talk about price with agencies._

**Primary anchor: headcount replacement.** The biggest cost isn't tool subscriptions — it's people. Agency payroll runs 50–60% of revenue. Every hour of manual work is a direct cost. Berlin automates 70% of that manual work across unlimited clients. Frame it against what they'd pay to hire 1–3 more SEO specialists ($60K–$120K/year each), not against what they pay for Ahrefs.

**Secondary anchor: per-client economics.** Agencies think in per-client profitability. At $499/month with unlimited projects, the per-client cost of Berlin drops with every client they add. At 10 clients, it's ~$50/client/month. At 20 clients, ~$25/client/month. At 50 clients, ~$10/client/month. And each client's manual work burden drops by 70%. This is the math that makes agencies scale.

**Tertiary anchor: tool consolidation.** Agencies spend $500–$2,000+/month on fragmented tools. Berlin replaces most of that stack. The consolidation savings alone can offset a significant portion of the subscription cost — and that's before counting the hours saved.

**Pricing structure:**

- **Founding Partner (current):** $499/month flat, unlimited projects, locked in forever. This is the agency Founding Partner rate. Includes full platform access, all integrations, workflow marketplace, brand context per client, team management, plus a 2-hour AI agent training session, 1-month hands-on onboarding, and direct roadmap influence. This price never increases for Founding Partners.
- **Phase 2:** $800–$1,000/month. As the Strategy Layer matures — industry-specific SEO strategies from experimentation across verticals that make agents progressively smarter.
- **Phase 3:** $1,500–$2,500/month. Full platform maturity with compounding intelligence across agency verticals.

Founding Partners lock in $499/month permanently and get every future capability at no additional cost.

**Internal justification angle:** The champion (Agency Owner or Director of SEO) is usually also the decision-maker at agencies, which shortens the sales cycle. Arm them with: (1) the headcount math — "70% of manual work automated, equivalent to 1–3 hires," (2) the per-client economics — "per-client cost drops to near zero as you scale," (3) the proof point — "3 agents, 2 hours, 120 hours saved," (4) the competitive urgency — "other agencies are already pitching AI search capabilities," (5) the pricing urgency — "$499 is the Founding Partner rate; it goes to $800–$1,000 in Phase 2."

## 9. Tone & Voice for This ICP

- **Practical and ROI-driven.** These are business owners and operators. They care about margin, capacity, and growth. Lead with numbers and outcomes, not features. "120 hours saved" lands harder than "AI-powered workflow automation."
- **Respect the grind.** Agency life is real — the late-night reporting, the client calls asking about things you don't have answers for yet, the constant pressure to do more with less. Acknowledge this reality without being patronizing. Berlin isn't a silver bullet — it's the infrastructure that makes the work manageable.
- **No buzzword AI hype.** Agencies have been pitched "AI tools" that don't work. They're skeptical. Be specific about what Berlin does differently and why it's reliable. Show, don't claim.
- **Speak in their language.** "Clients," "accounts," "deliverables," "proposals," "audits," "retainers" — not "workflows" and "pipelines" (unless referring to specific product features). Match how they describe their own work.
- **Urgency without fear-mongering.** The competitive pressure is real — agencies that can't operationalize AI search services will lose clients. But frame it as opportunity, not doom. Berlin lets them be the agency that has answers, not the one scrambling to catch up.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Always anchor messaging in specific examples. "Build an agent that turns a prospect URL into a branded SEO audit in 5 minutes" is more powerful than "automate your agency workflows."
