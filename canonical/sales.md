## What Berlin Is (Sales Positioning)

Berlin is two co-equal things: an **end-to-end AI SEO/AEO platform** the customer's team operates on, and a **dedicated marketer (FDM)** who executes on it with them. The user talks to a dashboard; Berlin strategizes, sets up execution, and executes — and an embedded expert operator drives it alongside the team. The mental model is three pillars — **Strategy / Execution / Tools** — and Berlin owns all three.

**Berlin sits in the middle of a spectrum** (⚠️ the canonical version of this framing was removed on 2026-07-28 pending AEO re-derivation — see `product.md` → *Positioning*. What follows is sales' own working copy, not canonical, and is expected to change):

- **Left — tool buyers** (Ahrefs, Profound, + an agent): skilled SEO operators who want leverage and do the work themselves. Berlin adds the operator and the execution a tool won't do.
- **Right — agency buyers** (Daydream and a thousand others): teams with no SEO function, or too small to build one, who hand it off. Berlin adds a platform the team operates on, transparency, and pace.
- **Middle — Berlin:** a platform *and* an expert operator, together. Our buyer is SEO/marketing-literate and wants both.

- The user can optionally bring their own strategy and let Berlin execute.
- Internal orchestration/inference stack is **internal infrastructure**. It is not a standalone product and should not be pitched as one.
- When prospects compare Berlin to a pure tool (Ahrefs, SEMrush, Profound), don't dismiss the comparison — use the spectrum: those give a skilled operator leverage; Berlin gives platform leverage *plus* an FDM who does the execution. When they compare Berlin to an agency, "replace your agency" still lands — the wedge is **FDM + agents**: typical agencies lag in tech and have no agents, so Berlin delivers the agency's outcome with a platform the team operates on, more transparency, and far more pace.

### One-line pitch

> "Berlin is a platform your team operates on *plus* a dedicated marketer who executes on it with you — the middle ground between buying a tool and hiring an agency. The platform handles strategy, execution, and the tooling underneath; the marketer drives it alongside your team in a space moving too fast to keep up with solo."

## Pricing Concessions for Budget-Sensitive Prospects

When a budget-sensitive prospect won't close at $499 outright, two trial structures are available:

1. **Free month + 2 prepaid** — first month free, customer prepays months 2 and 3 at $499.
2. **3 months at $299, then $499** — discounted ramp; price bumps to standard at month 4.

Use these sparingly and only when the prospect is a strong fit for the SaaS starting hypothesis.

## Qualifying Questions — Fit to the Starting Hypothesis

A good discovery call does four things: confirms the prospect is **SaaS**, confirms they're **already doing SEO with a small team/solo founder and some AI**, identifies the **buyer sub-type** (founder vs marketing leader), and maps the **current state** (DIY / agency / in-house / tools). It also gathers what we need to **narrow the ICP**.

### 1. Vertical & SEO maturity — is this our SaaS hypothesis?

> "Tell me about the business — what do you sell, and how do you make money?"
> "What's your SEO looking like today — how much are you doing, and are you using any AI tools for it?"

Confirm **SaaS**. If eCommerce, Lead Gen-Services, marketplace, publisher, aggregator, or pure agency — out of scope for this phase. Confirm there's **real SEO activity** (often AI-assisted) run by a **small team or solo founder**. No SEO activity yet = not the hypothesis.

### 2. Buyer sub-type — Founder or Marketing Leader?

> "What's your role in the business, and who owns SEO today?"

| Founder tier ($499–$999) | Marketing Leader tier ($2.5k–$5k) |
|---|---|
| "I'm the founder, I do it myself" | "I'm the Head of Marketing / CMO / Demand Gen Lead" |
| "We're 2–15 people, no dedicated marketer" | "We're 20–100+, I have a team / agency already" |
| "I sign the checks personally" | "I have a budget line for SEO" |
| Willing to sit in the product | Wants it done, wants reports, wants accountability |

If ambiguous, ask directly: "Do you want to run SEO yourself with Berlin doing the heavy lifting, or do you want Berlin to run it and just show you results?"

### 3. Current state — what's in place today?

> "What's happening with SEO right now — who does it and how?"

| State | Signal | Positioning angle |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| **Nothing / DIY** | Founder doing it at night, blog posts occasionally | Berlin replaces the need to hire; you get strategy + execution from day one |
| **Freelancer(s)** | "Hired someone on Upwork," inconsistent results | Berlin is more reliable than a freelancer and includes strategy |
| **Agency** | $3k–$10k/mo retainer, unhappy, opaque reporting | **The core wedge.** Berlin replaces the agency at comparable or lower cost, with more transparency and faster iteration |
| **In-house SEO person/team** | Dedicated hire(s) | Berlin augments them; or, at earlier stage, replaces the need for the next hire |
| **Tools only** | Ahrefs/SEMrush subscriptions but no execution | Those are tools *inside* Berlin's stack — we go further, all the way to execution |

### 4. Trigger — why now?

> "What's making you look at this now rather than six months ago or six months from now?"

**Good triggers:** Agency contract renewal coming up · Traffic/ranking drop (often AI-overview related in 2026) · AI search anxiety · Funding round → growth targets · Competitor outranking them · Freelancer/agency burn, no trust left.

**Bad triggers:** "Just exploring" · "Someone mentioned you" · "Curious about AI."

### 5. Budget sanity

> "What are you spending on SEO today, across tools, freelancers, and agency or in-house?"

- Founder tier: should be spending $200–$2,000/mo today. If $0, low likelihood of closing at $499–$999.
- Marketing Leader tier: should be spending $3k–$15k/mo on agency + tools. That's the budget Berlin redirects.

### 6. Decision authority

> "If this looks like a fit, who needs to be involved to say yes?"

Founders should say "me." Marketing Leaders should have budget authority in their tier. Anyone else is a proxy — insert yourself into the real decision-maker's next conversation.

## Call Structure & Framework

### Pre-call prep (5 min)

LinkedIn / company site review → confirm SaaS + SEO activity, pre-classify buyer sub-type (founder vs marketing leader). Hypothesize trigger and current state.

### 1. Opening & Rapport (3–5 min)

Thank them, confirm time, light rapport. If they open with "Tell me about Berlin" or "What's the price," redirect:

> "Happy to get to that — let me ask a few things first so the answer's relevant to your situation rather than generic. Fair?"

### 2. Discovery (12–15 min)

Run the qualifying sections in order: vertical & SEO maturity → buyer sub-type → current state → trigger → budget → decision authority.

### 3. Positioning & Demo (8–10 min)

Lead with outcomes, not features. Anchor on what the prospect just told you.

**Core positioning line:**
> "You told me [X]. Here's what that looks like in Berlin: [dashboard conversation → Berlin proposes strategy → you approve → Berlin executes]. The thing you'd otherwise do with an agency or an in-house hire, Berlin does end-to-end, and you see every step."

**Three-pillar framing** — Strategy (Berlin proposes a plan; you can accept, modify, or bring your own) · Execution (Berlin writes, ships, updates, monitors — actual published output) · Tools (rank tracking, crawl, entity analysis, AI-overview monitoring all included).

**Demo flow:**
1. Start at the dashboard conversation — show the prospect asking Berlin about *their* business.
2. Show Berlin producing a strategy artifact.
3. Show one concrete execution output (published page, internal linking change, schema update) tied to their pain point.
4. Close the loop: "So instead of briefing an agency and waiting two weeks, that just happened in the conversation."

Check comprehension every 2–3 minutes. If they're lost, simplify.

### 4. Closing (3–5 min)

See closing section below.

## Persona-Specific Approaches

Two buyer sub-types inside the SaaS starting hypothesis. Both already do SEO, often with AI, on a small/solo team — lead with that shared reality.

### SaaS × Founder ($499–$999 anchor)

- **Pain anchors:** Doing SEO themselves with AI tools and hitting a ceiling — quality, time, or AI-search visibility. Losing organic discovery to ChatGPT/Perplexity; no bandwidth to keep pace.
- **Lead with:** "You're already doing the SEO yourself with AI. Berlin is the platform plus a dedicated marketer who takes it the rest of the way — without the cost or onboarding of a hire."
- **Emphasize:** AI-search visibility, getting past the DIY-with-AI ceiling, time back, no-hire execution.
- **Watch for:** SEO isn't always top-3 priority; may stall. Create urgency around AEO/AI-overview decay.

### SaaS × Marketing Leader ($2.5k–$5k anchor)

- **Pain anchors:** Small team already running SEO (often AI-assisted) and capacity-bound; or an underperforming agency with no AI story; slow reporting.
- **Lead with:** "Your team is already doing real SEO. Berlin gives them a platform that does the volume plus a dedicated marketer who runs it with them — more output, full visibility, no retainer lock-in."
- **Emphasize:** Transparency, speed-to-publish, AEO coverage, augmenting a small team rather than replacing it, no-renewal lock-in.
- **Watch for:** If an agency is in place, the political lift of switching — offer a parallel-pilot path.

## Common Objections & Responses

### Positioning objections

**"How is this different from hiring an SEO agency?"**
> "An agency gives you people who use tools to do SEO work — and most agencies are behind on the tech; they don't have agents. Berlin gives you a platform with AI agents doing the volume, plus a dedicated marketer who runs it with you. The same output an agency promises — strategy, content, technical fixes, reporting — but faster, fully transparent in a dashboard you operate, and without the retainer lock-in."

**"Why wouldn't I just hire an in-house SEO lead?"**
> "A good in-house SEO lead is $120k–$180k fully loaded, takes three months to ramp, and still needs tools. Berlin replaces that hire's output on day one at [tier price]. When you eventually hire, Berlin makes them 3–4x more productive, so it's not wasted."

**"Can I still run my own strategy?"**
> "Yes, that's a supported path. You bring the strategy, Berlin executes and reports. Most customers start with Berlin proposing strategy and gradually take more of it on themselves — or stay in full auto, depending on their style."

**"What about the tools I already pay for — Ahrefs, SEMrush, Clearscope?"**
> "Those are fine — they're tools. Berlin includes the equivalent capabilities in the platform, so most customers drop them within 60 days. If you want to keep Ahrefs for its link index, you can; Berlin integrates with it."

**"Isn't this just a wrapper on GPT / Claude doing SEO?"**
> "No. Berlin has its own orchestration, its own ranking-signal stack, its own execution pipelines. LLMs are one component of many. The differentiator is that Berlin actually ships the work — writes, publishes, updates, monitors — not just generates suggestions."

**"How do I know the output quality matches what an agency would produce?"**
> "Fair question. Two answers: one, we'll show you production output from comparable SaaS customers on the call. Two, Berlin's output is reviewable — you approve before anything ships, or you don't, depending on how hands-on you want to be."

**"We have an agency contract until [date]."**
> "Good to know. Two paths: we can run Berlin in parallel for the last 60 days of your contract so you have a clean comparison before you renew, or we start the day your contract ends. Which fits better?"

### Standard objections

**"Pricing concerns"**
> "Compare it to what you're spending today. [Founder tier:] You're probably spending $500–$2,000 on tools and a freelancer. Berlin replaces all of that and goes further. [Marketing Leader tier:] You're likely on a $3k–$10k agency retainer. Berlin is the same spend or lower, with more output."

**"Can I just try it and cancel?"**
> "Month-to-month, no lock-in. What I recommend: we agree on 2–3 specific metrics upfront — say, AI-overview presence and organic conversions — set them up in week one, review at week four. That way you're measuring, not guessing."

**"Do you have a free trial?"**
> "No, because SEO results take time — a 7-day trial doesn't show real value. We're month-to-month instead. Let's set success metrics upfront and review at week four. If you're not seeing the trajectory, you cancel."

**"How do you ensure output quality / representative analysis?"**
> "We don't rely on single-shot LLM output. Berlin runs thousands of query variations, cross-checks against real ranking data and AI-overview presence, and every execution is reviewable before it ships if you want it to be."

**"We prefer human-only work."**
> "Berlin is human-directable at every step — you approve strategy, you approve output, you approve anything we publish. The AI does the heavy lifting; you keep judgment. Most customers settle into a mix where strategic calls are theirs and execution is Berlin's."

## Closing & Next Steps

### Standard close (preferred)

> "Based on what you've shared, I think [tier] fits. Here's what I recommend: we start [specific date]. Before then, you connect GSC, GA, and [CMS]. First week you'll see [specific output]. By week four, we review [agreed metrics]. Does [date] work, or is [alt date] better?"

### Proof-of-value close (use for skeptical but qualified)

> "Give me one page or one topic area and your biggest SEO pain around it. I'll have Berlin produce the strategy and one execution artifact within a week. We reconvene, you see real output on your actual site. If it's useful, we talk contract. If not, you got a free audit. Fair?"

**Rules:** Always book the follow-up before doing the work. One page / one topic / one artifact. No scope creep. Don't offer this more than 3× per week.

### Design-partner close (strong-fit SaaS prospects only)

> "You're exactly the profile we're learning from right now. I'd like to invite you into our design-partner program — [X]% off locked in, direct input on roadmap, weekly call for 8 weeks. In exchange we need real feedback and willingness to actually run SEO through Berlin. Interested?"

### What makes a strong close

- **Specific start date** — not "soon"
- **Clear prerequisites** — what they connect / send before kickoff
- **Binary scheduling** — "Tuesday or Wednesday?"
- **Agreed success metrics** — no ambiguity at week 4

### Weak closes to avoid

- "I'll send you an email"
- "Let me know when you're ready"
- "Sounds good, talk soon"
- Letting prospect defer to an internal meeting without you

### Tiers

- **Founder anchor — $499–$999 / mo.** SaaS founder owns SEO. Self-serve-leaning with FDM onboarding.
- **Marketing Leader anchor — $2,500–$5,000 / mo.** SaaS marketing leader with a small team. FDM-led, higher-touch.
- **Design-partner discount.** 30–50% off list, locked in, for high-priority discovery prospects only.

(The full scope ladder was removed from canonical on 2026-07-28 pending re-derivation for the AEO direction — see `product.md` → *Pricing*. Do not quote tier numbers from memory until it is rebuilt.)

### If they push back on price

1. Don't discount casually — discount only via the design-partner lane or the budget-sensitive concession structures.
2. Reframe to what they're replacing — agency retainer, tool stack, in-house hire.
3. Connect to a specific outcome they mentioned.
4. Confirm fit before negotiating — if they don't fit the SaaS hypothesis, price is a symptom, not the issue.

### Never say

- "We're still figuring out pricing"
- "We're early-stage so..."
- "It's expensive for us to run"
- Any statement that implies uncertainty

### Using price increases for urgency

Only if genuinely planned, and always paired with a CTA:
> "List is moving to $X next quarter. If you start before [date], you lock in today's rate. Want me to hold that?"

## Critical Mistakes to Avoid

### Positioning

- Don't pitch Berlin as a workflow builder, a "programmable infrastructure," or a tool you build on top of. That's the old positioning. Berlin is a **co-equal platform + FDM**.
- Don't pitch internal infrastructure (USOL etc.). It's internal.
- Don't flatten Berlin to either end of the spectrum. We are neither "just a better tool than Ahrefs/Profound" nor "just another agency." Use the middle: a platform the team operates on **plus** an FDM who executes on it. Tools (Ahrefs, SEMrush, Profound) are the left edge, agencies the right edge — Berlin is the middle, not a peer of either. (Agency-replacement is still a live wedge — just win it on **FDM + agents**, the tech agencies lack, not on being "cheaper.")
- Don't use "FDM" or "AEO" in prospect-facing copy. Use "dedicated marketer" and "search."

### Discovery

- Don't skip qualification. Every prospect must fit the SaaS starting hypothesis (SaaS, real SEO activity, small team/solo founder, literate owner, ~$80k–$1M MRR) before you demo.
- Don't skip budget. Ask plainly: "What are you spending on SEO today, across everything?"
- Don't skip the trigger. **No trigger = no close.**
- Don't accept a proxy buyer.

### Demo

- Don't over-explain architecture. The prospect cares about outcomes, not entity graphs or orchestration.
- Don't lead with features. Lead with outcomes tied to what they said in discovery.
- Don't volunteer incompleteness. If asked directly about a gap, be honest; don't unprompted.
- Don't peer-talk technology with technical prospects. Stay on business outcomes.

### Pricing

- Don't hedge. State the tier, connect to value, stop.
- Don't discount without the design-partner frame or the budget-sensitive concession structure.
- Don't justify price with your costs.

### Closing

- Don't close vaguely.
- Don't let prospects defer to internal meetings without you.
- Don't skip booking the next action on the current call.

## What Good Discovery Looks Like

- **Phase 1 — Current state (4 min):** Business, role, SEO today.
- **Phase 2 — Pain & trigger (4 min):** What hurts, what changed, why now.
- **Phase 3 — Fit & sub-type (3 min):** Confirm SaaS + SEO activity, confirm buyer sub-type (founder vs marketing leader), map tool and spend landscape.
- **Phase 4 — Decision mapping (2 min):** Who signs, what's the process, what's the timeline.
- **Phase 5 — Success criteria (2 min):** What does "working" look like at 30 and 90 days.

### Red flags — when to disqualify

- Doesn't fit the SaaS starting hypothesis (non-SaaS vertical, pure agency, no real SEO activity, below ~$80k MRR)
- $0 current SEO spend with no trigger
- Proxy buyer with no path to the real decision-maker
- Unrealistic expectations ("#1 ranking in 2 weeks")
- Wants bespoke workflow building — that's the old positioning

## Learning Log

> Entries from before the April 2026 repositioning have been pruned. Surviving entries generalize to the new motion. Items flagged with `*` were learned under the old positioning — sanity-check before applying.

- **Redirect opening questions.** When a prospect opens with "tell me about Berlin" or "what's the price," redirect with permission.
- **Always ask about budget.** "What are you spending on SEO today, across everything?" is the cleanest version.
- **Always ask 'why now?'.** Without a trigger, there's no close.
- **Don't volunteer incompleteness.** If asked directly about a gap, be honest — but don't unprompted.
- **Insert yourself into the next conversation.** Never let a proxy buyer carry your pitch into the decision-maker's office.
- **Comprehension checks during demo.** Every 2–3 minutes: "Does this map to how you'd use it?"
- **Backlink honesty.\*** Berlin's backlink automation is limited. If a prospect's pain is primarily backlinks, acknowledge briefly and pivot to what Berlin does strongly.
- **Don't talk about our internal journey.\*** Prospects don't care about agency→SaaS, LLM experiments, or rebuild history.
- **Educational lectures are a trap.\*** Keep any explanation to 1–2 sentences, then pivot to outcome.
- **Peer conversation trap.\*** With technical prospects, don't stay in tech discussion. Stay on business value.
- **Concrete timelines, not vague ones.\*** Never say "compound effects." Say: "Month 1: foundation. Month 3: 20–30% improvement on [metric]."
- **State price, then connect to value.\*** "$X/mo. That replaces [what they're spending today] and delivers [outcome they mentioned]."

### Flagged for review (under old positioning, may not generalize)

- Agency-specific workflow probes — built for the old agency ICP.
- Founding Partner exclusivity language — replaced by design-partner program.
- Tech-savvy agency owner / internal dev team sub-segments — those self-selected under the old workflow-builder positioning.
