## The FDM Model

### What an FDM Is

**FDM = Forward Deployed Marketer**, a concept Palantir popularized in their early enterprise days. In Berlin's model, the FDM is the **human half of a co-equal platform + FDM offering** — a senior marketer embedded alongside the platform, executing on it with the customer, handling everything the agents can't, and owning the relationship. Product and FDM carry equal weight; the FDM is not a layer bolted onto a product, nor the product a substrate beneath the FDM. (⚠️ The canonical statement of that co-equal framing lived in `product.md` and was removed on 2026-07-28 with the rest of positioning, pending AEO re-derivation — see `product.md` → *Positioning*. This file is now the only place the co-equal claim is asserted; whether it survives the repositioning is open.)

For end customers (the Berlin + FDM offering), the FDM is **part of the core offering, not a premium add-on**. The agency-tier product (Berlin for Agencies) is the only version sold without an FDM — the agency provides that layer themselves.

### Why FDMs exist

The agent has gotten genuinely good at the SEO/AEO work — but failure points remain, clustered around setup and judgment:

- **Every client is different** — different stage, different business, different position in the SEO/AEO timeline.
- **Setting Berlin up correctly for a given client is where most failure modes show up** — token spiking, hallucinations, edge cases, missing the actually-important signals because they're buried in too much data.
- **The FDM is the human layer that closes those gaps** — they configure Berlin for the client's real situation, review output before it ships, and own the relationship the buyer expects from an agency.

The FDM also produces a compounding effect: every engagement surfaces patterns about what strategy works for which customer profile, and those patterns flow back into Berlin's default playbooks.

### Operator ratios — where the 1/4-of-agency-cost pricing comes from

| Model | Marketer-to-client ratio |
|---|---|
| Typical SEO agency | 1 marketer handles **4–8 clients** |
| Berlin + FDM | 1 FDM handles **10–15 clients** at the quality of a senior agency operator |

The new surface area for SEO/AEO has roughly 10x'd the workload. Berlin's engineering substrate (Snake.blue crawl, cached data layer, proprietary data routing architecture, ranking-signal coverage) is what lets the human ratio scale **up** instead of down — and that's where the **~1/4 of agency cost pricing anchor** comes from. It's a unit-economics outcome of the substrate, not a discount strategy.

### Two delivery modes

The FDM engagement adapts to whether the customer has internal marketing:

1. **Companies with internal marketing teams.** The FDM works alongside the team and upskills them. Once the team is self-sufficient, the customer can move to a lower tier and operate Berlin themselves.
2. **Companies without an internal marketing function.** The FDM acts as the full marketing function on top of Berlin.

### Customer-facing language

In prospect-facing copy, write **"dedicated marketer"** or **"senior strategist."** Never "FDM" or "Forward Deployed Marketer" externally. Internal docs, hiring materials, and investor materials may use the acronym.

## First FDM Hire — Role Spec (Q2 2026)

The first SEO Strategist (FDM) is the template for the next ones. Role framing matters because the hiring funnel and the FDM motion are both being defined.

| Parameter | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Title (external)** | SEO Strategist |
| **Title (internal)** | Forward Deployed Marketer (FDM) |
| **Seniority** | **Mid-level (2–4 yrs hands-on SEO).** Explicitly not super-senior — wants someone who has seen what's possible without the "baggage of learned techniques" given how much SEO has changed. |
| **Book size** | 6 clients |
| **Scope** | Pure execution / delivery. **No pre-sales, no pitching new business.** |
| **Location** | Fully remote |
| **ICP coverage** | SaaS (the chosen first vertical — see `customers.md`) |
| **Beyond execution** | Build playbooks per client type; run AEO/LLM-visibility experiments; document learnings; feed Berlin product feedback and feature requests |
| **Cadence** | Weekly client calls minimum |
| **Application** | jobs@agentberlin.ai — recent SEO experiment, one thing traditional SEO folks are getting wrong about AEO/LLM, LinkedIn or resume. No cover letters. |

**Velocity is the job.** Berlin is not a typical agency. There is no fixed monthly deliverable list. Search visibility is moving faster than wildfire — AI overviews, LLM citations, answer engines, new ranking signals every quarter — and the only way to keep up is to run fast: apply fast, verify fast, kill what doesn't work fast, and do dramatically more for each client than a traditional agency would in the same window. **Berlin is what makes that pace possible — and the FDM uses Berlin exclusively for execution.**

## Hiring Philosophy

- **Mid-level over senior** for FDM hires — wants people who have seen modern SEO without the legacy baggage of pre-AI playbooks.
- **Generalists over specialists** at FDM level — technical, on-page, content, off-page all in one person.
- **Velocity > polish.** The job is shipping experiments and reading results, not perfecting one playbook.
- **Loud, constructive product feedback is part of the role.** FDMs complain when Berlin gets something wrong; that becomes the roadmap.
